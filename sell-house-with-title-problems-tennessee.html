Why a title problem stops a normal sale

"The house is still in Mom's name and nobody knows how to sell it." "My uncle owns a share and we haven't heard from him in years." "The title company found a mortgage from 1998 that was paid off but never released." If any of that sounds familiar, you're not stuck forever, but you probably can't sell the usual way.

Almost every buyer needs title insurance, and a buyer's lender requires it. Before a title company issues a policy, it searches the county records. If it finds a gap, a missing signature or an unpaid lien, it won't insure the title until that's cleared. No title insurance usually means no loan, no closing and no sale. Fixing that gap is called curative title work.

Common Tennessee title problems

  • The owner died and the house is still in their name. When someone dies without a will, Tennessee real estate vests in the heirs at the moment of death, but it can still be used to pay the estate's debts (Tenn. Code § 31-2-103). A title company will want proof of who the heirs are, or a probate estate, before it insures a sale.
  • Heirs property with many heirs. When a house passes down a generation or two without probate, ownership splits among more and more relatives. Each one owns a share, and each one has to sign, or a court has to act.
  • Missing or uncooperative heirs. One sibling won't answer, one lives overseas, one can't be found at all.
  • Old mortgages that were never released. The loan was paid off years ago, but no release was ever recorded.
  • Judgment and IRS liens. A court judgment or a federal tax lien attached to the owner's property. The IRS can issue a certificate of discharge for a specific property when it's sold (IRS Publication 783).
  • Deed errors. A wrong legal description, a misspelled name, a missing signature or a deed that was never recorded.
  • Tax and code liens. See selling with tax liens in Tennessee.

The Tennessee tools that clear title

You don't need to learn how to use these. It helps to know they exist, because they're why most title problems have an answer:

  • Probate: the probate court appoints a personal representative who can deal with the estate. In Nashville that's the Davidson County Probate Court; in Memphis, the Shelby County Probate Court.
  • Small estate affidavit: Tennessee's small estate process covers personal property only, up to $50,000, so it doesn't transfer a house (§ 30-4-102).
  • Heirship records and deeds from heirs: documenting who inherited and getting each heir's signature.
  • Quiet title: a lawsuit asking a court to declare who owns the property.
  • Partition: Tennessee's partition law lets a co-owner ask a court to divide or sell the property (Title 29, Ch. 27).
  • Tennessee's heirs property act: for partition cases filed on or after July 1, 2022 involving family-inherited property, the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act adds a court-set value and a chance for co-owners to buy out the others before any sale (§§ 29-27-301 to -313).
  • Releases, payoffs and corrective deeds: for old mortgages, liens and paperwork mistakes.

What a title problem can cost you while it waits

The title doesn't get cleaner with time. More heirs get added as family members pass away. Property taxes keep coming due and, once delinquent, draw 1.5% interest a month (§ 67-5-2010). An empty house can draw code violations. Starting the cure sooner usually means fewer signatures and lower costs.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell a house that's still in my deceased parent's name in Tennessee?
Usually, yes, once the right people can sign. In Tennessee, real estate passes to the heirs at death when there's no will, but a title company will want the heirs documented or a probate estate opened before it insures the sale. Talk to a Tennessee real estate attorney, and send us the address so we can look at the title.
What if one heir won't sign?
Every owner normally has to sign a deed. If one heir refuses or can't be found, the options include buying out that share, a quiet title case or a partition case, and Tennessee's heirs property act gives co-owners a chance to buy out the others before any court sale. We can tell you which paths might work, usually within 24 hours.
Can a small estate affidavit transfer a house in Tennessee?
No. Tennessee's small estate process covers personal property only, up to $50,000, so it doesn't transfer real estate. The house usually needs probate or other heirship steps.
What is Tennessee's heirs property law?
Tennessee adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act in 2022. For partition cases filed on or after July 1, 2022 involving qualifying inherited property, the court sets a value and lets co-owners buy out the interests of those who want a sale.
Can every title problem be cleared?
No, and we won't pretend otherwise. Most can, but some take a court case and months of work, and a few can't be cleared at a cost that makes sense. We'll tell you honestly what we find.
Do you buy houses with IRS liens or old unreleased mortgages?
Yes. An old paid-off mortgage usually needs a recorded release, and an IRS lien can often be paid or discharged from the sale at closing. Send us the details and we'll explain how it would work.
