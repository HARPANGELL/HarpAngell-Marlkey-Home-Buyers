Facing Foreclosure in Atlanta? Know Your Options Before the First-Tuesday Sale

Georgia is one of the fastest foreclosure states in the country, and there’s no buying the house back afterward. Here’s how the Atlanta process works, where to find your sale, and what you can still do before that Tuesday.

The short answer: Georgia forecloses out of court. The lender must mail you notice at least 30 days before the sale and advertise it once a week for four weeks in the county’s legal newspaper. The sale happens on the first Tuesday of the month, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., at the county courthouse — 136 Pryor Street SW for Fulton, 556 N. McDonough Street in Decatur for DeKalb. Georgia law gives no right to buy the house back after the sale, so the time to act is before it.

Atlanta homeowners are often surprised by how little time a Georgia foreclosure takes once it starts. There’s no lawsuit and no judge reviewing the sale beforehand — just a mailed notice, four weeks of newspaper ads and a first-Tuesday auction. That speed is exactly why it pays to understand the steps now, while you still have room to move.

Most of the city of Atlanta is in Fulton County, with part in DeKalb. A judge can move a county’s sale location, so always go by the place printed in your own sale ad.

The Georgia timeline, step by step

  1. Day 1 to 120. Missed payments, late fees and servicer calls. For most home loans, federal rules generally keep the servicer from starting foreclosure until you’re more than 120 days behind (12 CFR §1024.41(f)).
  2. At least 30 days before the sale. The lender mails you a notice of the sale. It must name the person with full authority to negotiate, amend or modify your loan, with an address and phone number (O.C.G.A. §44-14-162.2). Call that person.
  3. Four weeks before the sale. The sale is advertised once a week for four weeks in the county’s legal newspaper (§44-14-162; §9-13-140).
  4. The first Tuesday. The house is sold at the courthouse by public outcry between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. (§9-13-161).
  5. After the sale. No right to buy the house back. If the lender wants a deficiency, it must get the sale confirmed by a judge within 30 days and prove the house brought true market value (§44-14-161).

For the month-by-month detail, see How Long Does Foreclosure Take in Georgia? and What Happens After a Foreclosure Notice in Georgia?

Options to raise before the first Tuesday

  • Reinstate: ask the servicer for a written figure to bring the loan current, including fees.
  • Repayment plan, forbearance or loan modification: the person named in your 30-day notice is who to ask. Send a complete application as early as you can; it gives you the most protection.
  • Sell: a sale that closes before the auction pays off the loan, and the equity left after liens and costs is yours.
  • Short sale or deed in lieu: if you owe more than the house is worth.
  • An attorney: if you’re considering bankruptcy or think the notice or ads were defective.

Free Atlanta help: the Atlanta Legal Aid Society’s Home Defense Program gives advice, referrals and representation to homeowners at risk of losing their homes in Fulton, DeKalb, Cobb, Gwinnett and Clayton counties (Fulton office 404-524-5811; DeKalb office 404-377-0701). It’s also a HUD-approved housing counseling agency. HUD’s counselor line is 800-569-4287, and foreclosure counseling is always free.

Questions homeowners ask

When and where are foreclosure sales held in Atlanta?
On the first Tuesday of the month between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. (O.C.G.A. §9-13-161). For Fulton County that’s at the Fulton County Courthouse, 136 Pryor Street SW; for DeKalb County it’s the DeKalb County Courthouse, 556 N. McDonough Street, Decatur. If the first Tuesday is New Year’s Day or July 4, the sale moves to Wednesday. Your sale ad names the exact spot.
How much notice do I get before a Georgia foreclosure sale?
At least 30 days. The lender must send written notice by certified or registered mail or overnight delivery, and it has to name the person with authority to negotiate or modify your loan (O.C.G.A. §44-14-162.2). The sale is also advertised once a week for four weeks in the county’s legal organ.
Can I get my house back after a Georgia foreclosure sale?
Generally no. Georgia gives no right of redemption after a non-judicial foreclosure sale. You can stop the process before the sale by paying what’s owed, working out an agreement with the lender, or selling, but once the sale happens the house is gone.
Can the lender come after me for the rest of the loan?
Only if it follows an extra step. To pursue a deficiency, the lender must report the sale to a superior court judge within 30 days and get it confirmed, and the court has to find the house sold for its true market value (O.C.G.A. §44-14-161). You get notice of that hearing. If you receive one, talk to an attorney.
Can I sell my house if it’s already advertised for foreclosure?
Yes. You own the house until the sale, and a sale that closes before the first Tuesday pays off the loan so there’s nothing left to auction. Georgia closings run through an attorney and need a title search and payoff, so the sooner you start, the better.
