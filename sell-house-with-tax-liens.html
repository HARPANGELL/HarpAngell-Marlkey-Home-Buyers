Back taxes pile up quietly, then all at once

Property taxes are easy to fall behind on, especially if the mortgage was paid off and there's no escrow, if you inherited the house, or if money got tight. The bills keep coming, penalties and interest stack up, and eventually the county moves to collect by selling the house or a lien on it. If that's where you are, you're not out of options, and you don't have to come up with the money first.

How a tax problem turns into losing the house

StateHow unpaid property taxes are collected
GeorgiaThe county tax commissioner can issue a tax execution (fi. fa.) and the house can be sold at a tax sale.
FloridaUnpaid taxes are sold as tax certificates; a certificate holder can later apply for a tax deed sale.
TexasThe taxing units can file a tax suit, and the house can be sold at a sheriff's or constable's tax sale.
TennesseeDelinquent taxes go to the county's delinquent tax attorney and a court-ordered tax sale.

Each state has its own deadlines, interest rates and redemption rights. Pick your state above for the details.

Property tax liens vs. IRS liens

A property tax lien comes from the county for unpaid property taxes on the house. A federal tax lien comes from the IRS for unpaid income or other federal taxes, and it attaches to everything you own. Both can usually be handled at closing, but an IRS lien may need a payoff or a release from the IRS first. If you have either kind, tell us up front and we'll factor it in.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell my house if I owe back taxes?
Yes. The back taxes, penalties and interest are paid from the sale proceeds at closing. You don't need to pay them before you sell.
Who pays the back taxes when a house is sold?
The taxes are paid out of the sale at closing by the title company, which reduces the seller's proceeds. The buyer doesn't take on old taxes after closing.
Can the county sell my house for unpaid property taxes?
Yes. Every state we buy in has a process for selling property, or a lien on it, for unpaid taxes. The timing and redemption rights differ by state.
Can I still sell my house after a tax sale?
Sometimes, if your state gives you a redemption period and you're still inside it. The redemption amount is paid from the sale. Tell us the date of the tax sale and we'll look at it.
What if I also have a mortgage or an IRS lien?
We can still make an offer. Everything is paid in order of priority at closing. Tell us about all the liens and we'll give you one clear number.
