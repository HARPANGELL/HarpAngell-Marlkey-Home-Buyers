Questions sellers ask

How do I sell an inherited house?
First, someone needs the legal authority to sell, which usually means probate or a court order that lets the heirs sell directly. Once that’s in place, you can list the house or sell it directly for cash. If you’re not sure where you stand, send us the address and we’ll tell you what we see.
Do I have to pay taxes when I sell an inherited house?
Inherited property generally gets a stepped-up basis, so heirs who sell near the home’s value at the time of death often owe little or no capital gains tax. Every situation is different, so confirm the details with a tax professional.
Can I sell an inherited house that needs repairs?
Yes. We buy inherited houses as-is, including outdated homes, deferred maintenance, and houses still full of belongings. Take the keepsakes and leave the rest; we handle the cleanout.
Can I sell an inherited house if I live out of state?
Yes. We can handle most of the process remotely, and closings can often be signed with a mobile notary near you. You don’t need to travel to sell.
What if the inherited house is still in my parent’s name?
That’s common, and it usually means probate or another legal step is needed before the house can be sold with clean title. A probate attorney can tell you which path fits your family. Tell us the situation, and we’ll take an honest look at the house and what a sale could look like.
