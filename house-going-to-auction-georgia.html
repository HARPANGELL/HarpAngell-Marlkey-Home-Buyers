Your Georgia House Is Going to Auction. Here's What You Can Still Do

Whether it's a first-Tuesday foreclosure sale or a sheriff's tax sale, you still own the house until the auction. Every day before then matters.

Seeing your address in the legal notices, or reading a letter with a sale date on it, is a gut punch. Whether the auction is a foreclosure or a tax sale, the feeling is the same: it's happening, and it's happening soon. Take a breath. What happens on auction day

Georgia auctions are in person, at the courthouse of the county where the house sits. In Atlanta, both foreclosure and sheriff's tax sales take place on the steps of the Fulton County Courthouse at 136 Pryor Street SW. DeKalb holds its tax sales at the courthouse at 556 N. McDonough Street in Decatur, and Gwinnett holds its tax sales in front of the Gwinnett Justice & Administration Center in Lawrenceville. Foreclosure sales in those counties are also held at the county courthouse; your notice and the legal ad name the exact spot.

At a foreclosure auction, the lender's attorney reads the notice and takes bids. The lender is often the only bidder and may buy the house itself. Winning bidders pay in cash or certified funds. Afterward, the new owner can file a dispossessory (eviction) action. If the sale didn't cover the debt, the lender can only chase a deficiency if it asks the superior court to confirm the sale within 30 days.

How little time is left

If you've received a foreclosure notice of sale, the auction can be as little as 30 days away, and the ads have already started. For a tax sale, the levy and four weeks of ads come before the sale date. A cash closing needs a title search, payoff figures and a signing. We can often close in about 14 days when title is clean, sometimes faster, but every day you wait takes options away. See my house is scheduled for foreclosure auction: what now?

What you can lose at auction

  • Your equity. Auction bidders are looking for a discount. Any equity above the debt only comes back to you as surplus if the bid is high enough, and you have to claim it. See what happens to your equity in a Georgia foreclosure.
  • Control of the timing. After a foreclosure sale, the new owner decides when you move.
  • Money if you redeem a tax sale. Getting the house back after a tax sale costs the bid plus a 20% premium in the first year.

What a fast cash sale can do before the auction

A sale that closes before the auction pays off the debt from the proceeds, so the auction doesn't go forward. For a foreclosure, the title company gets a payoff from the lender's attorney. For a tax sale, it pays the tax commissioner, and after a tax sale it can pay the redemption amount while the right to redeem is still open. You get what's left at the closing table.

That depends on a few things: the closing has to happen before the sale, the price has to cover the payoff (or the lender has to approve a short sale), and the title has to be insurable. We can't promise that every sale can close in time. If yours can't, we'll tell you quickly so you can put your energy into other options.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell my house before a foreclosure auction in Georgia?
Yes, as long as the sale closes before the auction. The closing pays off the loan from the proceeds, so the foreclosure sale doesn't go forward. If the price won't cover what you owe, the lender must approve a short sale. We can usually tell you within 24 hours whether the timing works.
What time are foreclosure auctions in Georgia?
Between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. on the first Tuesday of the month, at the courthouse of the county where the property is. If the Tuesday is a holiday, the sale moves to Wednesday. Your notice letter and the legal ad give the details.
Can I get my house back after a Georgia tax sale?
Usually, yes, for a while. You can redeem within 12 months of the sale, and after that until the buyer serves a barment notice ending the right. The cost is the bid plus taxes the buyer paid, plus a 20% premium for the first year and 10% for each year after.
What happens to the extra money after a Georgia auction?
After a foreclosure, surplus is held for whoever is legally entitled to it, which can include junior lienholders and you, and sometimes a court decides. After a tax sale, excess funds are held for the owner and lienholders, and notice goes out within 30 days. Either way, you have to claim it.
Is it too late if my auction is next week?
Maybe not, but it's tight. A closing needs a title search and payoff figures, so call or send the address today. If we can't close in time, we'll say so right away so you can call your lender, a housing counselor or an attorney.
Will the lender postpone the auction if I'm selling?
Postponing is the lender's decision, so ask early and get any postponement in writing. A complete loss-mitigation application sent more than 37 days before the sale generally stops the sale while it's reviewed.
