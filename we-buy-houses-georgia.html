Georgia at a glance

Georgia's rules for foreclosure and property taxes move faster than many states, which is why timing matters so much here.

TopicHow it works in Georgia
Foreclosure typeNon-judicial. Most loans use a security deed with a power of sale, so no lawsuit is needed.
When foreclosure can startFederal servicing rules generally bar the first foreclosure notice or filing until the loan is more than 120 days delinquent.
Notice before a foreclosure saleWritten notice at least 30 days before the sale, plus a newspaper ad once a week for four weeks in the county legal organ.
Foreclosure sale day and placeFirst Tuesday of the month, between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., at the county courthouse.
Who collects property taxesThe county tax commissioner. Taxes are generally due December 20 unless the county sets an earlier date or installments.
How property-tax sales workUnpaid taxes lead to a tax execution (fi. fa.), then a sheriff's tax sale at the courthouse, usually on a first Tuesday. The buyer gets a tax deed.
Redemption after a tax saleThe owner can redeem for 12 months, and after that until the buyer serves notice ending the right. The premium is 20% for the first year.
Typical foreclosure timelineWith 120 days of delinquency plus at least 30 days of notice, the earliest sale is roughly five months after the first missed payment.

Why Georgia sellers choose a cash sale

A cash sale isn't right for everyone, and we'll tell you so. If your house is in good shape, you have time, and you can cover repairs and a few months of carrying costs, listing with an agent may put more money in your pocket. A cash offer is usually less than what a fully repaired house could bring on the open market.

But a cash sale can make sense when time or condition takes the traditional route off the table:

  • Speed that fits Georgia deadlines. A foreclosure or tax sale date doesn't wait for a buyer's loan approval.
  • Certainty. No buyer financing to fall through and no appraisal surprises.
  • As-is. No repairs, no cleanout, no showings. Take what you want and leave the rest.
  • Problems handled at closing. Mortgages, back taxes and recorded liens are paid from the sale proceeds by the title company.

Frequently asked questions

How fast can I sell my house for cash in Georgia?
We usually make a written cash offer within 24 hours of getting the details. If the title is clear, we can close in as little as 14 days, or on whatever later date works for you. Tell us your deadline and we'll plan backward from it.
Do you charge fees or commissions to buy my Georgia house?
No. We charge no fees or commissions, and we pay typical closing costs. The offer we make is the number you work from, and the closing statement shows every payoff before you sign.
Can I sell my Georgia house if I'm facing foreclosure?
Usually, yes, as long as the sale closes before the foreclosure auction. The closing pays off the loan from the proceeds, so the sale doesn't go forward. Talk to your servicer and a free HUD-approved housing counselor too; we can usually tell you within 24 hours whether a cash sale can close in time.
Do you buy houses with back taxes, liens or code violations in Georgia?
Yes. We buy the house with those problems attached. Back taxes and recorded liens are paid from the sale proceeds at closing, and open code violations become our responsibility as the new owner.
What parts of Georgia do you buy houses in?
We buy across Georgia, including metro Atlanta, Savannah, Augusta, Columbus, Macon and Athens, and the smaller towns and rural counties in between. Send us the address and we'll confirm.
Am I obligated to sell if I ask for an offer?
No. Asking for an offer is free and confidential, and you can say no. You're welcome to compare our offer with listing or with your lender's options before deciding.
