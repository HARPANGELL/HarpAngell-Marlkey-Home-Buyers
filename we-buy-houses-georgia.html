How foreclosure works in Georgia

Georgia is a non-judicial foreclosure state: for most home loans, the lender uses the power of sale in the security deed instead of going to court. That makes it one of the fastest processes in the country.

  • The 120-day line. Federal rules generally keep a servicer from starting foreclosure until a home loan is more than 120 days delinquent (12 CFR §1024.41(f)).
  • Notice to you. At least 30 days before the sale, by certified or registered mail or overnight delivery, naming the person with authority to negotiate or modify the loan (O.C.G.A. §44-14-162.2).
  • Newspaper ads. Once a week for four weeks in the county’s legal organ (§44-14-162; §9-13-140).
  • The sale. The first Tuesday of the month, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., at the county courthouse (§9-13-161).
  • After the sale. No general right to buy the house back. To pursue a deficiency, the lender must get the sale confirmed by a judge within 30 days (§44-14-161).

Talk to your servicer and a free HUD-approved housing counselor (800-569-4287) about reinstatement, a repayment plan or a modification. If selling is the right move, start early: a sale that closes before the sale date pays the loan off at closing, but only if there’s time for the title work and payoff. More detail: how long foreclosure takes in Georgia and our Atlanta foreclosure guide.

Selling in Georgia: what’s different

  • Attorney closings. Georgia closings run through a Georgia closing attorney, even cash sales with no lender. See who pays closing costs in a cash sale.
  • Transfer tax. $1 for the first $1,000 of the price plus 10 cents for each additional $100. The seller is liable by law, but the contract can shift it, and in a sale to us it’s part of the typical closing costs we cover.
  • No standard disclosure form. Georgia doesn’t mandate one, but sellers still can’t hide known serious defects. See what as-is really means in Georgia.
  • Probate. Each county has its own probate court. Creditor notice alone means about four months of waiting (how long probate takes), and a surviving spouse or minor children may use a year’s support petition.
  • Out-of-state sellers. Mail-away closings are routine, and the 3% nonresident withholding can often be reduced. See selling a Georgia house from out of state.

Questions sellers ask

Do you buy houses anywhere in Georgia?
Yes. We’re headquartered in Atlanta and buy houses across Georgia, from metro Atlanta to Savannah, Augusta, Columbus, Macon, Athens and small towns in between. Send us the address and we’ll take a look.
How does foreclosure work in Georgia?
Most Georgia foreclosures happen out of court. The lender must send you written notice at least 30 days before the sale, advertise it once a week for four weeks in the county’s legal newspaper, and hold the sale on the first Tuesday of the month between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. at the county courthouse. There’s no general right to buy the house back after the sale.
Who handles the closing when I sell a house in Georgia?
Georgia is an attorney-closing state, so a Georgia closing attorney handles the closing even in a cash sale with no lender. When you sell to us, we cover typical closing costs, and you’ll see the estimated settlement statement before closing.
Can I sell a house that’s still in probate in Georgia?
Often, yes. Once the personal representative has authority (letters testamentary or of administration, plus a power of sale in the will or a court order), the house can be sold. A surviving spouse or minor children may also be able to use a year’s support petition, which has to be filed within 24 months of the death.
I live out of state. Can I still sell my Georgia house?
Yes. Georgia closing attorneys routinely handle mail-away closings. Georgia generally requires the buyer to withhold 3% of the price when a nonresident sells Georgia real estate, but a seller’s gain affidavit (Form IT-AFF2) can base it on the gain instead. Raise it early with the closing attorney.
