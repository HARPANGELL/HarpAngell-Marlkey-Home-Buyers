Facing Foreclosure in Dallas or Fort Worth? Know Your Options

Dallas–Fort Worth spans four big counties, and each one runs its own first-Tuesday sale in its own spot. Here’s how to find yours, how much time the Texas rules give you, and what you can still do about it.

The short answer: Most Texas home foreclosures are out-of-court trustee sales held on the first Tuesday of the month between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., at a spot each county designates near its courthouse. The notice of sale must be posted, filed with the county clerk and mailed to you at least 21 days ahead, after at least 20 days’ notice to catch up. Until the sale happens, the house is still yours and you still have options.

In DFW, the first question isn’t “how long do I have?” It’s “which county is my house in?” Texas sets the statewide rules, but each county picks where its monthly sale happens and where its notices get posted. Start there and the rest of the picture comes into focus fast.

Counting backward from the first Tuesday

Texas foreclosure runs on fixed minimums, so you can count backward from any sale date to see where you stand:

  • Sale day: the first Tuesday of the month, between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. The auction has to start at the time in your notice or within three hours after it (Property Code §51.002(a), (c)).
  • At least 21 days earlier: the notice of sale is posted, filed with the county clerk and sent to you by certified mail (§51.002(b)). If you’re reading a posted notice, you’re inside this window.
  • Before that: for a home you live in, the servicer must give you written notice of default by certified mail and at least 20 days to catch up (§51.002(d)).
  • Before any of it: federal rules generally keep a servicer from starting foreclosure until you’re more than 120 days behind (12 CFR §1024.41(f)).

Home equity loans, HELOCs and reverse mortgages are different: on a Texas homestead they need a court order before a sale (Texas Constitution art. XVI §50), usually through an expedited court process with a response deadline. If you’ve received court papers instead of a posted notice, read them right away.

What you can still do

Call your servicer and ask, in writing, for a reinstatement quote (the amount to bring the loan current) and whether you qualify for a repayment plan, forbearance or a loan modification. The earlier a complete loss-mitigation application goes in before the sale, the more protection it gives you. If the house is worth less than you owe, ask about a short sale or a deed in lieu. And if you think a notice was wrong or you’re weighing bankruptcy, talk to an attorney before the sale date.

Free DFW help: a HUD-approved housing counselor costs nothing (HUD’s line is 800-569-4287). The CFPB counselor finder lists local agencies offering mortgage delinquency counseling, including the Dallas County Home Loan Counseling Center and Housing Channel in Fort Worth. If you qualify by income, Legal Aid of NorthWest Texas handles foreclosure cases across Dallas, Tarrant, Collin and Denton counties (Legal Aid Line: 888-529-5277).

When selling is the better move

Sometimes the numbers just don’t work for keeping the house. If a sale can close before the first Tuesday, the loan gets paid off and whatever equity is left after liens and costs goes to you instead of disappearing at the auction. Questions homeowners ask

Where are foreclosure auctions held in Dallas County?
On the first Tuesday of the month, on the north side of the George Allen Courts Building facing Commerce Street, under the overhang at 600 Commerce Street, unless the Commissioners Court designates another spot. The earliest start is 10 a.m.; your notice lists the exact time.
Where are foreclosure auctions held in Fort Worth?
Tarrant County holds them on the first Tuesday at the base of the courthouse steps on the west side of the Tarrant County Courthouse, 100 W. Weatherford Street, downtown Fort Worth. Check your notice for the start time.
How do I find out if my house is on this month’s foreclosure list?
Search the county clerk’s posted notices. Dallas County posts notices filed since February 24, 2026 on its Public Search site (choose Foreclosure). Tarrant County posts them on its Public Search site too, Collin County has a foreclosure notice lookup, and Denton County has a Foreclosure Notices page. Notices are posted at least 21 days before the sale.
Can I sell my house once the foreclosure is posted?
Yes. You own the house until the trustee sale, and if a sale closes before it, the loan is paid off from the proceeds and any equity left after liens and costs goes to you. Timing is the hard part, since a closing needs a title search and a payoff from your servicer, so reach out as soon as you see a posting.
What happens if the house sells at auction for less than I owe?
The lender can sue for the difference within two years of the sale, but you can ask the court to credit the home’s fair market value instead of the auction price, which can reduce or wipe out the deficiency (Texas Property Code §51.003). Get legal advice if that letter arrives.
