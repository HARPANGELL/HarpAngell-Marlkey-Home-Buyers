A summons, a lis pendens in the county records, maybe a letter with a sale date. It's easy to feel like the house is already gone. In Florida, it usually isn't yet. Foreclosure runs through a courtroom, and every step has a deadline you can plan around.

How foreclosure works in Florida

Florida uses judicial foreclosure. State law says all mortgages are foreclosed "in equity," so your lender has to file a lawsuit in circuit court, and a judge, not a jury, decides the case (Fla. Stat. § 702.01). When the suit is filed, the lender records a lis pendens, a public notice that the property is tied up in a lawsuit (Fla. Stat. § 48.23). You can still sell with a lis pendens on file.

Federal rules come first. Your servicer generally can't make the first foreclosure filing until you're more than 120 days behind, and if you send a complete loss mitigation application more than 37 days before a sale, it generally can't move for judgment or hold the sale while it reviews you (12 CFR 1024.41).

What you stand to lose if nothing changes

  • Your equity. At the clerk's sale, bidders buy as-is and the price is whatever the auction brings. Money above the judgment becomes surplus that you have to claim; one year after the sale, unclaimed surplus is presumed unclaimed and sent to the state (§ 45.032).
  • A possible deficiency. If the sale brings less than you owe, the court may enter a deficiency judgment. For owner-occupied homes it's capped at the difference between the judgment and the home's fair market value on the sale date (§ 702.06).
  • Your redemption window. You can pay the judgment amount until the clerk files the certificate of sale. After that, the house is gone.
  • Time and fees. Every month adds interest, late charges and the lender's attorney costs to the payoff, which comes straight out of your equity.

Your options before the sale

Call your servicer to ask what it can offer, and consider a free HUD-approved housing counselor (HUD: 800-569-4287) to review it with you. The main paths:

  • Reinstate: pay the past-due amount, fees and costs to bring the loan current.
  • Repayment plan or forbearance: catch up over time, or pause payments for a while.
  • Loan modification: new loan terms to make the payment affordable.
  • Refinance: possible with enough equity, income and credit.
  • Sell: list with an agent if you have time, or take a cash offer if you need speed.
  • Short sale or deed in lieu: if you owe more than the house is worth, with lender approval.
  • Bankruptcy: can pause a sale but has lasting effects; talk to a Florida bankruptcy attorney.

How a cash sale before the auction works

A sale that closes before the clerk's auction pays off your loan from the proceeds, so the foreclosure sale doesn't go forward. It works when the closing happens in time and the price covers the payoff and other liens; if it doesn't, a short sale needs the lender's approval. Here's what happens with us:

  • The title company orders a written payoff from your lender, including missed payments, fees and the lender's attorney costs.
  • At closing, the payoff is wired from the sale proceeds and any other liens are paid. Whatever is left is yours.
  • We can close in as little as 14 days when title is clear, or on your schedule if the sale date allows.

The tighter the sale date, the more the title search and payoff timing matter, so tell us your date on the first call. If you're earlier in the process, see behind on mortgage payments in Florida.

Frequently asked questions

How long does foreclosure take in Florida?
There's no fixed length because Florida foreclosures go through the courts. Federal rules generally keep a servicer from filing until you're more than 120 days behind, you get 20 days to answer after you're served, and the clerk's sale is usually set 20 to 35 days after the final judgment. Send us your case number and we can usually tell you within 24 hours whether a sale can close in time.
Can I sell my house during foreclosure in Florida?
Yes. A lis pendens doesn't stop you from selling, and you can pay off the judgment until the clerk files the certificate of sale. The title company pays your lender from the sale proceeds at closing. Send us the address and we'll tell you what we can offer.
Can selling my house stop a foreclosure sale in Florida?
A sale that closes before the auction pays off the loan, so the foreclosure sale doesn't go forward. That only works if the closing happens in time and the price covers the payoff, or the lender approves a short sale. We'll tell you honestly whether your timeline is workable.
What happens to surplus funds after a Florida foreclosure sale?
If the house sells for more than the judgment and costs, the extra is surplus. Florida presumes the owner of record when the lis pendens was filed is entitled to it, after subordinate lienholders who file timely claims. One year after the sale, unclaimed surplus is presumed unclaimed and sent to the state, so claim it with the clerk promptly.
Should I talk to my lender or a housing counselor first?
Yes. Call your servicer to ask about reinstatement, a repayment plan or a modification, and consider a free HUD-approved housing counselor at 800-569-4287. Talking to us doesn't stop you from doing any of that; a cash number just gives you one more option to compare.
