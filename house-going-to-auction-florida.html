Is Your Florida House Going to Auction? Here's What You Can Still Do

Whether it's a clerk's foreclosure sale or a tax deed sale, the date is real and the window is short. Here's what happens and how a fast sale can fit before it.

Quick answerIn Florida, a foreclosure auction is a clerk's sale usually set 20 to 35 days after the final judgment, and a tax deed sale is held by the clerk after four weeks of published notice. Until the clerk files the certificate of sale in a foreclosure, or until a winning tax deed bidder pays the clerk in full, the debt can still be paid, and a cash sale that closes first pays it from the proceeds. After that, your main remaining right is to claim any surplus funds.

There's a date on the calendar, and the house you've lived in, inherited or rented out is scheduled to be sold to a stranger online. If that's where you are, the honest truth is that time is short. But until certain paperwork is filed after the auction, you still have rights, and there are still things that can happen before it.

Foreclosure auction: the clerk's sale

In Florida, a judge sets the foreclosure sale in the final judgment, usually 20 to 35 days out, and the clerk of court runs it as a public auction. A later date is possible only if the lender agrees. Notice runs for 2 consecutive weeks, with the last newspaper notice at least 5 days before the sale (Fla. Stat. § 45.031).

In the big metros the sale is online: Duval at duval.realforeclose.com (weekdays, first sale 11:00 a.m.) and Hillsborough at hillsborough.realforeclose.com (weekdays, 10:00 a.m.), with Pinellas, Orange, Miami-Dade and Broward on their own realforeclose sites. In Duval and Broward, bidders put down a 5% deposit, and properties sell as-is (Duval Clerk, Hillsborough Clerk, Broward Clerk).

Tax deed auction

A tax deed sale comes after years of unpaid property taxes, once a certificate holder applies for a tax deed (§ 197.502). The clerk publishes notice once a week for 4 consecutive weeks, and the sale can't happen until 30 days after the first notice (§ 197.512). The clerk mails notice, and the sheriff notifies the owner, at least 20 days before the sale (§ 197.522). Pinellas holds these online at pinellas.realtaxdeed.com from 11:00 a.m. (Pinellas Clerk), and Miami-Dade at miamidade.realforeclose.com (Miami-Dade Clerk).

You can redeem until a tax deed is issued, unless the winning bidder has already paid the clerk in full (§ 197.472). On homestead property, the opening bid includes half the assessed value, and that amount is treated as surplus. More on the tax side: selling with back taxes in Florida.

What you lose at the auction

  • Control of the price. Bidders decide what the house sells for, as-is, with no showings and no negotiation.
  • Your equity, unless you claim it. Surplus doesn't arrive on its own. In a foreclosure, the owner of record when the lis pendens was filed is presumed entitled, after subordinate lienholders who file timely claims (§ 45.032). After a tax deed sale, the clerk mails a surplus notice, and most claims are barred if not filed within 120 days of it, so file yours with the clerk promptly (§ 197.582).
  • Possibly more. If a foreclosure sale brings less than the judgment, the lender may seek a deficiency judgment (§ 702.06).

Be careful with anyone who asks you to sign over your surplus rights. The clerks in counties like Pinellas and Miami-Dade post owner claim forms (Pinellas Clerk, Miami-Dade Clerk).

What a fast cash sale can do before the sale date

A sale that closes before the auction pays off the foreclosure judgment or the back taxes from the proceeds, so the auction doesn't go forward. That only works if:

  • the closing happens before the sale;
  • the price covers the payoff and other liens, or the lender approves a short sale; and
  • the title can be cleared in time.

Not every sale date leaves enough time, and we'll tell you honestly if yours doesn't. When timing is tight, the foreclosing lender can agree to a later sale date, but it doesn't have to (§ 45.031). Call your servicer and a free HUD-approved housing counselor too; if you send a complete loss mitigation application more than 37 days before the sale, federal rules generally bar the servicer from holding the sale while it reviews you (12 CFR 1024.41). Frequently asked questions

Can I sell my house before the foreclosure auction in Florida?
Yes. You can pay off the judgment until the clerk files the certificate of sale, or a later time set in the judgment, and a sale that closes before the auction does that from the proceeds. The closing has to happen in time. Send us the sale date and we can usually tell you within 24 hours whether it's workable.
How do I find my foreclosure sale date in Florida?
It's in the final judgment in your case, and the clerk's online auction calendar for your county lists scheduled sales. Your county clerk's office can also look up the case. Once you have the date, send it to us with the address.
What happens after a foreclosure auction in Florida?
The clerk files a certificate of sale, which ends your right to redeem in most cases. If no objections are filed within 10 days, the clerk issues a certificate of title to the buyer. Any surplus above the judgment has to be claimed through the clerk.
Can a tax deed sale be avoided in Florida?
The taxes can be redeemed until a tax deed is issued, unless the winning bidder has already paid the clerk in full. A sale that closes before then pays the redemption amount from the proceeds. If your tax deed sale is coming up, contact the tax collector for the amount and send us the address.
What are surplus funds and who can claim them?
Surplus is the money left after an auction pays the debt and costs. In a foreclosure, the owner of record when the lis pendens was filed is presumed entitled after timely lienholder claims; after a tax deed sale, the clerk mails a notice and most claims must be filed within 120 days of it, so file promptly. Be cautious with anyone who wants you to sign your rights over.
Is it too late to sell if the auction is next week?
It may be. A cash sale still needs a title search and a payoff, and some dates don't leave enough time. Call us anyway; we'll tell you straight, and you should also call your servicer and a free HUD-approved housing counselor.
