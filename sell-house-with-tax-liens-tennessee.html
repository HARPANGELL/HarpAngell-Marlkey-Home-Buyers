How delinquent property taxes work in Tennessee

Maybe the tax bills piled up after a job loss, or the house belonged to a parent and nobody realized the taxes weren't being paid. Now there's a letter about a lawsuit. You still have time to sell, but the clock in Tennessee is real, and the cost grows every month.

In Tennessee, property taxes are collected by the county trustee. They're due the first Monday in October (§ 67-1-701), and in counties like Davidson and Hamilton you can pay without interest through the last day of February. On March 1 they're delinquent, and interest of 1.5% a month starts (§ 67-5-2010). That's 18% a year before any lawsuit costs.

  • Nashville: the Metropolitan Trustee is the tax collector for all of Davidson County.
  • Memphis: the Shelby County Trustee collects county property taxes and runs the county's tax sale schedule.
  • Knoxville: the Knox County Trustee collects county property taxes.
  • Chattanooga: the Hamilton County Trustee collects county taxes and taxes for several smaller cities. Chattanooga isn't on that list, so check for a separate city bill too.

Tennessee lets some cities collect their own property taxes (§ 67-1-701), so if you live inside city limits you may owe two bills.

From missed bill to tax sale

First Monday in October (year 1)Taxes due (§ 67-1-701)
March 1 (year 2)Delinquent; 1.5% monthly interest begins (§ 67-5-2010)
January (year 3)The trustee publishes notice that tax suits will be filed
February 1 to April 1 (year 3)The unpaid list goes to the county's delinquent tax attorney, who files suit by the last business day of March after the notice (§ 67-5-2405); a 10% penalty is added when the suit is filed (§ 67-5-2410)

After the saleA redemption period runs from the order confirming the sale (§ 67-5-2701)

So the lawsuit usually comes about a year after the taxes go delinquent, and the sale comes after that. That's time you can use, but interest, the 10% penalty and court costs keep growing the payoff.

Tax lien, tax certificate, tax deed or tax suit?

Tennessee property taxes are a first lien on the property. Unlike some states, Tennessee doesn't sell tax lien certificates; the Davidson County Clerk and Master says plainly that it doesn't issue them. Instead the county files a delinquent tax suit, usually in chancery court, and the court orders a delinquent tax sale. The buyer's title is subject to your right to redeem.

Redemption in Tennessee depends on how long the taxes went unpaid, not on whether it's your homestead:

Years the taxes were delinquentTime to redeem after the order confirming the sale
Five years or lessOne year
More than five, fewer than eight180 days
Eight or more90 days
Court finds the house vacant and abandoned30 days

Redeeming means paying the taxes, penalties, interest and costs plus 12% a year interest on the buyer's entire purchase price (§ 67-5-2701). Once the redemption period ends, the house is gone. If it sold for more than was owed, you can ask the court for the excess, but only after lienholders are paid and only by filing a motion (§ 67-5-2702). Unclaimed excess eventually goes to the state's unclaimed property program.

Can you sell a house with back taxes in Tennessee?

Yes. Back taxes don't stop a sale; they get paid from it. Here's how it works:

  1. The title company searches the title and finds every unpaid tax year, tax suit and other lien.
  2. It gets the payoff. Before a suit, that's from the trustee. After a suit is filed, payments go through the court clerk, and in Davidson County paying in full before the sale gets the suit dismissed.
  3. At closing, the taxes, interest, penalties and costs come out of the sale price first, and the lien is released.
  4. You get what's left after the taxes, any mortgage and other liens.

If the house has already been sold at a tax sale, you may still be inside a redemption window. That's tighter and more expensive. Talk to a Tennessee real estate attorney right away, and send us the details; we'll tell you honestly whether we can help.

Federal IRS liens are a separate thing

An IRS tax lien isn't a property tax lien. It's the federal government's claim against everything you own, including your house, after you don't pay a federal tax debt, and a Notice of Federal Tax Lien may be filed publicly. It can still be handled in a sale: the IRS can issue a certificate of discharge for a specific property (see IRS Publication 783), and the title company coordinates the payoff at closing.

Your options

  • Pay in full: the simplest route if you have the money.
  • Partial payments: Tennessee trustees may accept them (§ 67-5-1801), and Shelby County offers written payment plans of up to 24 months for houses scheduled for a tax sale. Interest keeps running.
  • Tax relief or tax freeze: state programs for qualifying older, disabled and veteran homeowners, handled through the county trustee.
  • Redeem after a sale: possible within the window above, at the 12% cost.
  • Sell: pay everything off at closing and keep the rest.

Frequently asked questions

When are property taxes delinquent in Tennessee?
Tennessee property taxes are due the first Monday in October and become delinquent on March 1 of the next year, when interest of 1.5% a month starts. If a tax suit is filed, a 10% penalty is added on top, plus court costs.
How long before Tennessee sells my house for back taxes?
The delinquent tax suit is usually filed about a year after the taxes go delinquent, and the court-ordered sale comes after the judgment. After the sale you may have up to one year to redeem, or less if the taxes were unpaid for more than five years or the house is found vacant and abandoned. We can usually make you an offer within 24 hours if you'd rather sell first.
Can I sell my house if it's in a delinquent tax lawsuit?
Yes. The title company gets the payoff through the court clerk and pays the taxes, interest, penalty and costs from the sale at closing. In Davidson County, paying in full before the sale gets the lawsuit dismissed.
Does Tennessee sell tax lien certificates?
No. Tennessee collects unpaid property taxes through a lawsuit and a court-ordered tax sale, not by selling tax certificates. The buyer at a tax sale takes the property subject to the owner's right to redeem.
Do you buy houses with IRS liens too?
Yes. A federal tax lien is separate from property taxes, but it can usually be paid or discharged through the title company at closing. Send us the details and we'll tell you how it would work for your house.
