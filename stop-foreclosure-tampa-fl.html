Foreclosure in Hillsborough County? Know the Online Sale Timeline

In Tampa, foreclosures end in an online auction run by the Hillsborough County Clerk — but only after a court case you can respond to. Here’s the timeline, how to track your case, and what you can still do.

The short answer: Florida forecloses through the courts, so a Tampa foreclosure starts with a lawsuit and you generally get 20 days to respond once you’re served. After a final judgment, the Hillsborough County Clerk sells the house online at hillsborough.realforeclose.com, weekdays at 10:00 a.m., usually 20 to 35 days later. You keep the right to pay off the debt and keep the house until the clerk files the certificate of sale.

Tampa homeowners usually find out about a foreclosure from a process server, not a sale notice on the door. That’s the Florida difference: the lender has to win a court case first. The case is your window. Here’s how it typically unfolds in Hillsborough County, start to finish.

The Hillsborough timeline, start to finish

  1. The 120-day line. For most home loans, federal rules generally keep a servicer from filing until you’re more than 120 days behind (12 CFR §1024.41(f)). Use that time to apply for help.
  2. The lawsuit. The lender files in circuit court and records a notice of lis pendens (Fla. Stat. §702.01, §48.23). Mortgages in Florida can only be foreclosed this way, in front of a judge.
  3. Your 20 days. After you’re served, you generally have 20 days to file an answer (Fla. R. Civ. P. 1.140). Missing it can mean a default and a quicker judgment.
  4. Final judgment and sale date. If the lender wins, the judge sets a sale 20 to 35 days later, or later if the lender agrees. Notice runs on a public website for two straight weeks or in a newspaper once a week for two weeks (Fla. Stat. §45.031).
  5. The online auction. The Clerk sells the house at hillsborough.realforeclose.com, weekdays at 10:00 a.m. Bidders put down a 5% deposit.
  6. Certificate of sale. Your right to redeem ends when the clerk files it, unless the judgment sets a later time (Fla. Stat. §45.0315). Objections are due within 10 days; then the Clerk issues the certificate of title.

Track each step on HOVER, the Clerk’s online records system, where the public can search case indexes and view dockets and filed documents. The Clerk’s foreclosure sales page explains the auction rules.

After the sale: your money and your exposure

Surplus funds. If the house sells for more than the judgment, the extra goes to the Clerk, and the owner of record is presumed entitled to it after any junior liens that filed claims on time. You can claim it yourself until the Clerk reports it as unclaimed, one year after the sale (Fla. Stat. §45.032). You don’t need a recovery company; if you use one, Florida caps its fee at 12% (§45.033).

Deficiency. If the sale doesn’t cover the debt, a lender may ask for a deficiency judgment. It’s up to the court, and for an owner-occupied home it’s limited to the judgment amount minus the home’s fair market value (Fla. Stat. §702.06). On a one- to four-family home, a separate deficiency action must be brought within one year (§95.11(6)(g)).

Help in Tampa before it gets that far

  • Your servicer: request a reinstatement quote and ask about a repayment plan, forbearance or a loan modification. Send a complete application as early in the case as you can.
  • A HUD-approved housing counselor: free mortgage-default counseling. The CFPB counselor finder lists Tampa agencies such as the Housing and Education Alliance (813-932-4663). HUD’s line is 800-569-4287.
  • Legal aid: Bay Area Legal Services serves Hillsborough County and has foreclosure help for homeowners. Helpline (813) 232-1343; intake (800) 625-2257, weekdays 9 a.m.–4:30 p.m.

Questions homeowners ask

When are Hillsborough County foreclosure sales held?
Every weekday, Monday through Friday, at 10:00 a.m., online at hillsborough.realforeclose.com. The date for your house is set in the final judgment, usually 20 to 35 days after it’s entered.
How do I check the status of my Tampa foreclosure case?
Use HOVER, the Hillsborough Clerk’s online records system, to search case indexes and view the docket and filed documents. The final judgment shows the sale date, and upcoming sales are listed on the Clerk’s auction site.
Can I stop the sale by paying what I owe?
Florida law lets you redeem by paying off the debt before the clerk files the certificate of sale, or before a later time set in the judgment. After that, there’s no right of redemption. Talk to your servicer early about reinstatement or a payoff figure.
Do I need a company to recover my surplus funds?
No. Your final judgment includes a notice that you can claim surplus funds yourself without a lawyer, and it tells you to check with the Clerk within 10 days after the sale. Surplus-recovery companies are allowed, but Florida caps their fee at 12% (§45.033), and you can file a claim directly until the Clerk reports the money as unclaimed one year after the sale.
Can I sell my Tampa house while the foreclosure is pending?
Yes. You still own it until the sale. A sale that closes before the clerk’s auction pays off the loan and the case is dismissed, and you keep whatever equity is left after liens and costs.
