What Happens to Your Equity in a Georgia Foreclosure?

The equity you built doesn't simply come back to you after a foreclosure sale. Here's where the money goes, and what you can do before and after.

Published August 15, 2026 · Updated October 26, 2026 · MarKey Home Buyers

Here’s the part most people don’t hear until it’s too late: the equity you spent years building doesn’t automatically come back to you after a foreclosure sale. Some of it can, but only what’s left after everyone ahead of you is paid — and you usually have to claim it.

Where foreclosure sale money goes

When a Georgia house sells at a foreclosure sale, the proceeds generally go, in order, to:

  1. the costs of the sale;
  2. the foreclosing lender’s full balance, including missed payments, late fees and allowed legal costs;
  3. junior liens, such as second mortgages, HELOCs, judgments and tax liens.

Only what’s left after all of that — the “surplus” or “excess funds” — belongs to the former owner, and you typically have to claim it.

Why the surplus is often smaller than you expect

At a foreclosure sale, the price is set by whoever bids that day. Bidders are usually investors who price in repairs, risk and the fact that they can’t inspect inside, so bidding may never get near what the house would bring on the open market. Often the lender itself is the high bidder at roughly what it’s owed. Meanwhile, your payoff grows with interest, late fees and legal costs the longer the process runs. A house with plenty of equity on paper can produce a much smaller surplus, or none.

And if the sale doesn’t cover the debt

If the sale brings less than you owe, the lender can’t simply come after you for the rest. Under O.C.G.A. §44-14-161 it must report the sale to a superior court judge within 30 days and get it confirmed, and the court has to find that the house sold for its true market value. You get notice of that hearing. If one arrives, talk to an attorney.

Protecting your equity before the sale

  • Keep the house: a reinstatement, repayment plan or modification keeps the equity yours. Ask your servicer early, and talk to a free HUD-approved housing counselor (800-569-4287).
  • Sell first: a sale that closes before the foreclosure sale lets you agree to the price, stops the fees from growing at closing, and pays your equity to you at the closing table, with no claims process. Even a discounted cash sale can leave you with more than a foreclosure sale would — compare your actual numbers both ways before deciding.

If the sale already happened

Ask the foreclosing attorney whether there was a surplus and how to claim it. Be careful with “recovery companies” that offer to claim it for a large cut — you can often make the claim yourself, and an attorney can help if there’s a dispute.
