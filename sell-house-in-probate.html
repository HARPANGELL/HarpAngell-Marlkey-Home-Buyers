Questions sellers ask

Can you sell a house during probate?
Usually, yes, and you often don’t have to wait for the estate to close. Depending on the will and the powers the probate court granted, the executor or administrator may be able to sell right away or may need the court’s permission first. Your probate attorney can confirm what’s required, and we’ll work around that timeline.
Who has the authority to sell a house in probate?
Generally, the personal representative appointed by the probate court: the executor under a will, or an administrator if there’s no will. Until someone is appointed, there’s usually no one who can sign for the estate. We can still look at the house now, so you know your options when the time comes.
How long does probate take?
It depends on the state, the county and the estate. In Georgia, a simple estate often takes six months to a year, and contested or complicated estates can take much longer. The good news is that the house can often be sold before probate is fully finished. We can make an offer now and close when the estate is ready.
Do I have to clean out a probate house before selling it?
No. Take what the family wants to keep and leave the rest, and we handle the cleanout after closing. We buy the house as-is, so there are no repairs or showings either.
Do all the heirs have to agree to sell a house in probate?
It depends on who holds title and what authority the court has given. If the estate still owns the house, the personal representative can often sell with the proper authority, and heirs may get a chance to object; once the house belongs to the heirs together, every co-owner usually has to sign. Your probate attorney can tell you which applies, and we’re glad to work with them.
