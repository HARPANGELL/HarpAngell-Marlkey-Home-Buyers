Two kinds of Tennessee auctions

Seeing your address in a legal notice, or a date circled on a certified letter, is frightening. Two things to know right away. First, until the auction actually happens, the house is still yours, and you can still sell it. Second, Tennessee has two very different kinds of auctions, and the rules depend on which one you're facing.

What happens on auction day

At a trustee sale, the trustee offers the house at the time and place in the notice, or on the bidding website it names, and sells to the highest bidder. The lender can usually bid up to what it's owed. The winner gets a trustee's deed. If you're still living there, the new owner can move to take possession.

At a tax sale, the clerk sells under the court's order, with the opening bid set at the taxes, interest, penalties and costs. In Nashville, the Davidson County Chancery Court Clerk and Master holds in-person public auctions, usually once a month from June to December. In Memphis, the Shelby County Trustee posts the tax sale schedule and property lists, with bidding through an online auction site. In Chattanooga, the Hamilton County Clerk and Master runs the sale for county and City of Chattanooga taxes; a recent notice set it in the Commission Room of the Hamilton County Courthouse, 625 Georgia Avenue. The buyer's title is still subject to your right to redeem.

How much time is left

  • Trustee sale: the first newspaper ad can run as little as 20 days before the sale. Sales can be postponed for up to a year; postponements are posted online, and if a delay is more than 30 days you must get a new mailed notice at least 10 days before (§ 35-5-101(e)).
  • Loss mitigation: if your servicer gets a complete application more than 37 days before the sale, it generally can't hold the sale until it decides (12 CFR 1024.41(g)). Inside that window, the protection may not apply.
  • Tax sale: you can pay before the sale. In Davidson County, paying what's owed before the sale gets the lawsuit dismissed. After the sale, the redemption clock runs, and it gets more expensive every month.

What's at stake: your equity

At an auction, the house goes to the highest bidder, and that bid doesn't have to match what the house is worth. Equity above the debt comes back to you only if a bid leaves a surplus, and only if you claim it. After a trustee sale, if the price doesn't cover the loan, the lender can sue for the difference, generally within two years (§ 35-5-117). After a tax sale, getting the house back costs the taxes and costs plus 12% a year on the buyer's whole bid. Selling before the auction is usually the way to keep the most of what's yours.

What a fast cash sale before the auction can do

If a sale closes before the auction, the title company pays off the loan or the delinquent taxes from the sale price at closing, so the auction doesn't happen for that house. You get whatever is left after the payoffs.

That depends on one condition: enough time to close. We can close in as little as 14 days, but the title company needs a payoff statement and title clear enough to insure. If your sale is days away, we'll tell you honestly whether we can close in time. Whether a lender agrees to postpone a sale while a closing is pending is up to the lender. We can't promise a sale will be called off; we can promise a straight answer, fast.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell my house before the foreclosure auction in Tennessee?
Yes. You own the house until the auction, so you can sell it, and the loan is paid off from the sale at closing. The question is whether there's time to close before the sale date. Send us the address and the date and we'll usually tell you within 24 hours.
How much notice do I get before a Tennessee foreclosure auction?
The first newspaper ad must run at least 20 days before the sale, with a second ad, a 20-day online posting, and a copy mailed to you by the first ad date. Postponed sales must happen within a year, and a delay of more than 30 days requires a new mailed notice at least 10 days before.
Can I get my house back after a Tennessee tax sale?
Usually, for a limited time. You can redeem within one year of the order confirming the sale, or 180, 90 or 30 days in some cases, by paying the taxes, costs and 12% a year interest on the buyer's bid. After that, the house is gone.
What happens to extra money from a Tennessee auction?
After a trustee sale, any surplus left after the debt, costs and other liens is yours, but you have to ask the trustee for it. After a tax sale, excess proceeds are claimed by filing a motion in the court case, and lienholders are paid first.
Will a pending sale stop the auction?
Not automatically. A sale that actually closes before the auction pays off the debt, so the auction doesn't happen for that house. Whether a lender postpones while a closing is pending is the lender's decision, so tell us your date right away.
