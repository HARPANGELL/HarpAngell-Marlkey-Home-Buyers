Selling an Inherited House in Florida

When a family member dies, the house can feel locked in place. Here's how Florida probate and homestead rules work, and how we can buy it as-is.

Quick answerTo sell an inherited house in Florida, someone usually needs legal authority from the probate court in the county where your loved one lived. That may be formal administration, where the court appoints a personal representative, or summary administration if the estate is $150,000 or less (not counting exempt property) or the person died more than two years ago. Someone you loved has died, and now there's a house. Maybe it's full of belongings. Maybe the mortgage and tax bills keep coming in a name that isn't yours, and nobody in the family is sure who's allowed to sell. That "locked in" feeling is common, and in Florida there's a clear path out.

Do you need probate to sell an inherited house in Florida?

Usually yes, if the house was in the person's name alone. Probate is the court-supervised process that gathers a person's assets, pays debts and passes what's left to the heirs (The Florida Bar). It's handled by the court in the county where your loved one lived (Fla. Stat. § 733.101), such as Duval, Hillsborough, Pinellas, Orange, Miami-Dade or Broward. Property owned jointly with survivorship rights generally passes outside probate.

A personal representative who lives outside Florida can serve only if closely related to the person who died, such as a child, parent, sibling, niece or nephew (§ 733.304). Out-of-state heirs don't have to fly in for every step; we handle most of our side by phone, email and mail.

Florida homestead rules can change who owns the house

If the house was the person's homestead, Florida's rules can override the will:

  • If the owner left a spouse and children, the spouse generally gets a life estate and the children get the house after that, unless the spouse elects within 6 months of the death to take a one-half interest instead (§ 732.401).
  • A homestead can't be left by will to someone else if the owner is survived by a spouse or a minor child, though it can go to the spouse if there are no minor children (§ 732.4015).
  • Homestead property is generally not treated as a probate asset, but a title company may still want a court order determining homestead before it insures a sale.

Homestead questions are where a Florida probate or real estate attorney is worth the call.

When heirs disagree or live far away

Every owner has to sign. If one heir wants to keep the house and another wants to sell, Florida's Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act gives the court a structured process: a court-set value, a chance for the others to buy out the sellers, and a preference for an open-market sale over an auction (Fla. Stat. §§ 64.201-64.214). A lawsuit among relatives is slow and costly, so a firm cash number on the table often helps a family agree. For deeper problems, see selling a house with title problems in Florida.

The bills don't stop during probate

  • Mortgage: payments are still due. A lender generally can't call the loan due just because the house passed to a relative at the borrower's death (12 U.S.C. § 1701j-3), but missed payments can still lead to foreclosure. See behind on payments in Florida.
  • Property taxes: due November 1 and delinquent April 1 (FL Dept. of Revenue). If the new owners don't qualify for homestead, the assessment generally resets to full value the January 1 after the change of ownership (§ 193.155). See back taxes in Florida.
  • Insurance: tell the insurer about the death and whether the house is empty, and check the policy's conditions for vacant homes.
  • Upkeep and code issues: overgrown yards and empty houses draw code complaints, and Florida code fines can run daily.
  • Creditors: most claims against an estate are barred 2 years after death (§ 733.710), so heirs can't always distribute everything right away.

Your options

  • Keep it: one heir buys out the others or moves in.
  • Rent it: brings in income, but also landlord work and repairs.
  • List it: may bring more if the house shows well and the family can wait and pay for clean-out and prep.
  • Sell for cash, as-is: no clean-out, repairs or showings, and one closing for every heir.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell a house in probate in Florida?
Yes. A personal representative appointed by the court can sell the house. If the will gives a power of sale, court approval of the sale isn't needed; otherwise the court must authorize or confirm it. We can make an offer now and close when the estate is ready.
How long does probate take in Florida?
The Florida Bar says a simple estate takes about five or six months, and creditors generally have three months after notice is published to file claims. Disputes, homestead questions or a real estate sale can stretch that out. We work around the estate's timeline.
Do all heirs have to agree to sell an inherited house in Florida?
If the heirs already own the house together, every owner has to sign a normal sale. If they can't agree, Florida's Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act gives the court a process with a buyout option. A firm cash offer often helps families settle on a plan; we're happy to give you one.
Can I sell an inherited house without probate in Florida?
Sometimes. A house owned jointly with survivorship rights generally passes outside probate. Otherwise, you'll usually need formal administration, summary administration or, for a homestead, a court order determining who inherited it. Talk to a Florida probate attorney about which applies.
Who pays the mortgage on an inherited house in Florida?
The loan still has to be paid, usually by the estate or the heirs. Federal law generally bars a lender from calling the loan due just because the house passed to a relative at death, but missed payments can still lead to foreclosure. If no one can keep up, a sale pays the loan off at closing.
Do I have to clean out the house before selling?
Not with us. Take what matters to your family and leave the rest. We buy the house as-is, and we can usually make an offer within 24 hours.
