Selling a House With Back Taxes or Tax Liens in Florida

Florida runs on tax certificates and tax deeds, and the interest doesn't wait. Here's where you stand and how a sale can pay off what's owed.

Quick answerYes, you can sell a Florida house with delinquent property taxes. Taxes become delinquent April 1, the county tax collector sells a tax certificate on the debt by about June 1, and the certificate holder can apply for a tax deed once two years have passed since April 1 of the year the certificate was issued. Until a tax deed is issued, the taxes can be redeemed, and in a sale the title company pays them from your proceeds at closing.

The tax bill got put off one year, then another. Now there are letters about a tax certificate, or worse, a notice that someone has applied for a tax deed. It can feel like the county is about to take the house. In Florida there's a set path before that can happen, and you can sell at almost any point along it.

How delinquent property taxes work in Florida

Each county's tax collector collects property taxes, and the clerk of court runs tax deed sales. In the big metros that means the Duval, Hillsborough, Pinellas and Orange County Tax Collectors, the Miami-Dade County Office of the Tax Collector and the Broward County Tax Collector. Unpaid property taxes are a first lien on the property, ahead of other liens, from January 1 of the tax year until they're paid (Fla. Stat. § 197.122).

Tax lien, tax certificate, tax deed: what's the difference?

  • Tax lien: the county's claim on the property for unpaid taxes. It attaches automatically.
  • Tax certificate: at the annual sale, an investor pays your delinquent taxes and earns interest. Bidders compete by asking for the lowest rate, and certificates nobody buys are struck to the county (§ 197.432). The holder doesn't own your house.
  • Tax deed: the step that can actually cost you the house. After the waiting period the certificate holder applies, and the property is auctioned to the highest bidder (Broward County Tax Collector).

How long you have to redeem

You can redeem a tax certificate any time after it's issued and before a tax deed is issued, unless someone has already paid the clerk in full for the tax deed (§ 197.472). Redeeming means paying the taxes plus the certificate's interest (with a 5% minimum on most certificates) and costs. The amount can change month to month, so the figure has to come from the tax collector.

Homestead vs. non-homestead: the redemption window is the same. The difference shows up at the tax deed sale: on homestead property the opening bid must include half of the latest assessed value (§ 197.502), and that extra amount is treated as surplus (§ 197.582). Florida's homestead protection from forced sale doesn't cover property taxes (Fla. Const. art. X, § 4).

Where tax deed sales happen

Many clerks hold them online. Pinellas runs them at pinellas.realtaxdeed.com, starting 11:00 a.m. on scheduled sale days (Pinellas Clerk), and Miami-Dade at miamidade.realforeclose.com (Miami-Dade Clerk). Broward's tax collector notes it can take up to 16 months after an application before a sale is scheduled. If you already have a date, see house going to auction in Florida.

What you lose if nothing changes

  • Interest and costs keep building. Certificates can carry interest up to 18% a year, and each new unpaid year can add another certificate.
  • Ownership, at a tax deed sale. Bidders set the price. Anything above the opening bid becomes surplus that you have to claim; the clerk mails a notice, and most claims are barred after 120 days, so file promptly (§ 197.582).
  • Your choice of buyer and timing. A sale on your terms is almost always a better outcome than an auction.

Can you sell a house with back taxes in Florida?

Yes. Back taxes are a routine item for a title company to clear. Here's how it works when we buy:

  • The title search shows every unpaid year, outstanding certificate and other lien.
  • The title company gets the redemption amount from the tax collector, and the taxes, interest and fees are paid from your sale proceeds at closing. Current-year taxes are typically prorated.
  • Other liens, like a mortgage or code enforcement liens, are paid the same way.
  • The certificates are redeemed, and you receive what's left.

The one hard deadline: the sale has to close before a tax deed is issued, or before a winning bidder pays the clerk in full.

Federal IRS tax liens are a separate issue

An IRS lien isn't a property-tax lien. It's the federal government's claim for unpaid federal taxes, and it reaches all of your property, including real estate (IRS). It has to be paid, released or discharged from the property for a sale to close, which is a separate process from your county taxes. Tell us up front if you have one, and we'll plan for it.

Your options

  • Pay or redeem through the tax collector. Broward, for example, accepts delinquent taxes only in full with certified funds (Broward).
  • Installment plan for future bills: if your estimated taxes are over $100, you can apply by April 30 to pay the coming year quarterly (§ 197.222). It doesn't cover past-due years.
  • Homestead tax deferral for qualifying homeowners, based on household income (§ 197.252).
  • Sell the house and pay everything from the proceeds at closing.

Frequently asked questions

How long can property taxes go unpaid in Florida before you lose the house?
Taxes become delinquent April 1, a tax certificate is sold by about June 1, and the certificate holder can apply for a tax deed once two years have passed since April 1 of the certificate year. The clerk then gives notice and holds a sale. You can redeem until a tax deed is issued; if you're near that point, we can usually tell you within 24 hours what we can do.
Can I sell my house with a tax certificate on it in Florida?
Yes. The title company gets the redemption amount from the tax collector and pays it from your sale proceeds at closing, which redeems the certificate. The sale has to close before a tax deed is issued. Send us the address and we'll make an offer with the taxes still owed.
What's the difference between a tax certificate and a tax deed in Florida?
A tax certificate is a lien an investor buys at the annual sale by paying your delinquent taxes; it earns interest but doesn't make the holder the owner. A tax deed transfers ownership after the certificate holder applies and the clerk auctions the property. The certificate is the warning; the tax deed is the loss.
Does homestead protect my house from a tax deed sale in Florida?
No. Florida's constitution doesn't protect homestead from forced sale for property taxes. Homestead does change the opening bid, which must include half of the latest assessed value, and that amount is treated as surplus for the former owner and lienholders to claim.
Can I get a payment plan for delinquent property taxes in Florida?
Florida's installment plan lets you prepay upcoming taxes quarterly if they're over $100, but it doesn't cover past-due years. Counties generally require delinquent taxes in full; Broward, for example, takes them only in full with certified funds. Ask your tax collector, and if paying in full isn't realistic, a sale can pay them at closing.
What happens to surplus money after a Florida tax deed sale?
If the property sells for more than what's owed, the clerk holds the surplus and mails notice to the people with an interest in the property. Most claims must be filed within 120 days of that notice, and valid liens are paid before the titleholder. File promptly, and be cautious with anyone who asks you to sign over your rights.
