Memphis specifics

  • Foreclosure. Most Tennessee residential foreclosures are handled outside of court. Since July 1, 2025, state law has required the sale to be advertised at least two times in a newspaper in the county, starting at least 20 days before the sale, along with an online posting that stays up for at least 20 continuous days. The borrower must also be mailed a copy by registered or certified mail by the first newspaper date.
  • Probate. Shelby County has its own Probate Court, with two judges' divisions and a separate Probate Court Clerk's office in downtown Memphis.
  • Property taxes. The Shelby County Trustee (shelbycountytrustee.com) bills and collects property taxes, and its site posts the tax sale schedule and properties up for sale.
  • Code enforcement. The City of Memphis Code Enforcement office, housed in the Division of Community Enhancement, enforces the International Property Maintenance Code, including minimum housing standards and rules on inoperable or abandoned vehicles. The division also handles code lien waiver requests.

Received a notice of sale? Call your lender about catching up or a repayment plan, and consider a HUD-approved housing counselor. If you decide to sell, send us the date so we can tell you honestly whether a closing can happen first.
