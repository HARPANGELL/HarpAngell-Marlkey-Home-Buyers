Foreclosure: Tennessee trustee sales

Most Tennessee foreclosures are trustee sales held out of court under the deed of trust. Since July 1, 2025, the sale must be advertised at least twice in a newspaper in the county, the first time at least 20 days before the sale, and also posted online by a registered third-party posting website for at least 20 continuous days. The trustee must mail you a copy of the notice by registered or certified mail on or before the first publication. The notice names the sale's location, which can be a physical place or, for an online sale, the bidding website. Under federal rules, foreclosure generally can't start until you are at least 120 days behind.

A sale that closes before the trustee sale pays off the loan, so the foreclosure sale doesn't happen, as long as the price covers your payoff and closing is finished in time. Call your servicer, and talk to a free HUD-approved housing counselor about reinstatement, a repayment plan or a loan modification. See foreclosure in Tennessee and behind on mortgage payments in Tennessee.

House going to auction

A Memphis house can go to auction two ways: a trustee sale or a Shelby County tax sale. Trustee sales can be postponed for up to a year, with postponements announced through the posting website, so check the online notice for the current date. At either auction, the price is whatever the winning bidder pays. Selling before the sale date is how you keep control of the price and your equity. See a Tennessee house going to auction.

Back property taxes: Shelby County Trustee

The Shelby County Trustee collects county property taxes. Under Tennessee law, unpaid county taxes pick up interest of 1.5% a month starting March 1. Delinquent parcels go to a tax sale, and Shelby County's sales use online bidding on the Zeus Auction website. Check the Trustee's posted schedule for the next sale date. If your parcel is scheduled for a current tax sale, the Trustee offers payment arrangements that must cover every delinquent year and run no longer than two years.

After a sale, the former owner, heirs or lienholders can redeem by petition in Chancery Court: one year if taxes were five years delinquent or less, 180 days for five to eight years, 90 days for eight or more, and only 30 days for vacant and abandoned property. The buyer is owed up to 12% a year interest. Owners and lienholders can also claim excess proceeds through the Chancery Court Clerk.

We buy houses with back taxes. The title company pays the Trustee from the sale proceeds at closing. See selling a Tennessee house with tax liens.

Code violations: Memphis Code Enforcement

City of Memphis Code Enforcement takes complaints through 311. An inspector checks the property and notifies the owner; with numerous violations, you get a mailed list and a Notice of Hearing. Follow-up inspections can lead to an extension, an order to vacate, or condemnation. If the owner doesn't comply, the City can take the case to the Shelby County Environmental Court at 201 Poplar Avenue, which hears housing cases and Neighborhood Preservation Act and receivership cases.

For a condemned structure, the owner gets 10 calendar days to demolish it before the City does, and the City's demolition costs are assessed against the property's taxes. We buy Memphis houses with open violations and condemnation orders. The violations come with the house, and liens are settled at closing. See selling a Tennessee house with code violations.

Title problems

The Shelby County Register of Deeds records deeds and trust deed releases, and runs a Fraud Alert program that tells you when something is filed on your property. Common Memphis problems: the house is still in a deceased parent's name, an old deed of trust was never released, or a past tax sale clouded the title. We review title before we make an offer and work through what we find with the title company, and we'll tell you up front if something will take longer.
