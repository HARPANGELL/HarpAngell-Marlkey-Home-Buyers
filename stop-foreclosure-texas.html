Texas foreclosures move fast. Here's how the trustee sale process works, what you can still do, and how a cash sale before the sale date can pay off your loan.

How foreclosure works in Texas

If you've opened a certified letter that says "notice of default" or "notice of sale," your stomach probably dropped. Take a breath. Until the sale actually happens, you still own the house, and you still have choices.

Most Texas home loans are secured by a deed of trust with a "power of sale." That lets the lender foreclose without a lawsuit, through a public auction called a trustee sale. The Texas State Law Library notes that judicial foreclosure is rare in Texas. The big exception is a home equity loan or reverse mortgage: the Texas Constitution says a home equity lien can be foreclosed only by court order, usually through the expedited process in Texas Rules of Civil Procedure 735 and 736. If yours is a home equity loan, talk to a Texas real estate attorney about your timeline.

The notices and the timeline

Federal servicing rules generally bar a servicer from making the first foreclosure notice or filing until you're more than 120 days behind (12 CFR 1024.41(f)). After that, Texas moves quickly:

StepWhat Texas law requires
Notice of defaultSent by certified mail for a home you live in; gives you at least 20 days to cure before a notice of sale can be given (Tex. Prop. Code § 51.002(d))
Notice of saleAt least 21 days before the sale: posted at the courthouse door, filed with the county clerk, and sent to you by certified mail (§ 51.002(b))
Sale dayFirst Tuesday of the month, between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m.; the first Wednesday if that Tuesday is January 1 or July 4 (§ 51.002(a), (a-1))
Start timeThe sale must start at the time in the notice or within three hours after it (§ 51.002(c))
After the saleNo general right to buy the house back after a mortgage trustee sale; if you stay, the new owner may file an eviction case

Add 20 days and 21 days together, and a sale can land roughly six weeks after a notice of default, depending on when the next first Tuesday falls. The notice of sale must also carry a statement for military servicemembers. If you or your spouse are on active duty, tell your servicer and an attorney right away.

Where and when sales happen in the big Texas metros

Every county sells in an area its commissioners court designates at or near the courthouse. Here's what the county offices list:

  • Dallas County: north side of the George Allen Courts Building, 600 Commerce Street, facing Commerce Street below the overhang. Notices are filed with the Dallas County Clerk.
  • Tarrant County (Fort Worth): base of the courthouse steps on the west side of the building at 100 West Weatherford Street.
  • Bexar County (San Antonio): west side of the Bexar County Courthouse, between the courthouse and the Paul Elizondo Tower.
  • Travis County (Austin): west steps of the Travis County Courthouse, 1000 Guadalupe Street.
  • Harris County (Houston): notices are filed with the Harris County Clerk, which lets you search them by sale date. Your notice names the exact location.

Your notice of sale lists the date and the earliest time the sale can begin. That date is your real deadline. If you're already counting days, our page on what happens when a Texas house is going to auction explains sale day itself.

Your options before the sale

  • Reinstate: pay the past-due amount, not the whole loan, to bring it current.
  • Repayment plan, forbearance or loan modification: through your servicer. A complete application sent more than 37 days before a sale generally keeps the servicer from selling while it's reviewed (12 CFR 1024.41(g)).
  • Refinance: if your credit and equity allow it.
  • Sell: list with an agent if there's time, or sell for cash to close faster.
  • Short sale: if you owe more than the house is worth, with the lender's approval.
  • Deed in lieu: give the house back to the lender by agreement.
  • Bankruptcy: can pause a sale, with long-term effects. Talk to a Texas bankruptcy attorney.

How selling before the sale works

When you sell, the title company orders a payoff statement from your servicer. At closing, the loan balance, past-due payments, fees and any other liens are paid from the sale price, and the lender releases its lien. A sale that closes before the trustee sale pays off the loan, so the foreclosure sale doesn't happen. If you're also behind on property taxes, those get paid the same way; see selling a Texas house with back taxes.

Timing is the condition that matters most. We can close in as little as 14 days, but title work and payoff letters take time, so the more days you have before the posted date, the more room there is. If the sale is close, tell us the date up front. We'll be honest about whether a closing can happen first. Whether the servicer agrees to postpone is up to the servicer.

Waiting has a real cost. At a Texas trustee sale, the house goes to the highest bidder, and nothing in the trustee-sale statute requires that bid to match what the house is worth. Equity you could have walked away with in a sale can disappear. If a bid is higher than the debt, the extra (excess proceeds) goes first to other lienholders, and what's left may be available to you, but you have to claim it from the trustee. And if the house sells for less than you owe, the lender has two years to sue you for the difference, called a deficiency (§ 51.003).

Frequently asked questions

How long does foreclosure take in Texas?
Texas is one of the faster states. Federal rules generally keep the first foreclosure notice or filing from coming until you're more than 120 days behind; after that, a notice of default gives you at least 20 days to cure and the notice of sale must go out at least 21 days before a first-Tuesday sale. If you have a date, we can usually tell you within 24 hours whether a cash sale can close before it.
Can I sell my house if it's already posted for a trustee sale in Texas?
Yes. You own the house until the sale happens, so you can sell it, and the title company pays off the loan from the proceeds at closing. Whether there's enough time depends on the sale date and the title. Send us the address and the date and we'll give you an honest answer.
Can I get my house back after a Texas foreclosure sale?
Generally no. Texas allows redemption only after certain kinds of foreclosures, such as property tax and some property owners' association foreclosures, not a typical mortgage trustee sale. That's why acting before the sale date matters. A Texas real estate attorney can tell you whether anything about your loan is different.
What happens to my equity if my Texas house is foreclosed?
The house goes to the highest bidder, and the winning bid doesn't have to match what the house is worth. Money left after the debt, costs and other liens are paid is excess proceeds that may be available to you if you claim it. Selling before the sale lets you keep whatever equity is left after the payoff.
Is foreclosure different for a home equity loan in Texas?
Yes. The Texas Constitution requires a court order to foreclose a home equity lien, usually through the expedited process in Texas Rules of Civil Procedure 735 and 736. That can change your timeline, so check with a Texas real estate attorney. You can still sell the house while it's pending.
Do you charge fees to buy a house in foreclosure?
No. We charge no fees or commissions and we pay typical closing costs. Your loan payoff and any liens come out of the sale price at closing, through the title company. We can usually have a cash offer to you within 24 hours.
