Facing Foreclosure in Jacksonville? Your Options Before the Clerk’s Sale

Florida foreclosures go through a judge, which gives Jacksonville homeowners more time than most states — if they use it. Here’s how a Duval County case moves, the local help most people never hear about, and where selling fits.

The short answer: Florida forecloses through the courts. The lender has to file a lawsuit, you get 20 days to respond after you’re served, and only after a final judgment does the Duval County Clerk sell the house — online, on weekdays, usually 20 to 35 days after the judgment. Until the clerk files the certificate of sale, you still own the house and still have options.

If you’ve been served foreclosure papers in Jacksonville, the most important thing to know is that a Florida foreclosure is a lawsuit, not an automatic countdown. Nothing gets sold until a judge enters a final judgment. That gives you a real window to catch up, negotiate, get help, or sell on your own terms — but only if you respond.

How a Duval County foreclosure moves

Final judgmentThe judge sets a sale 20 to 35 days out (later only if the lender agrees). Notice runs on a public website for two straight weeks, or in a newspaper once a week for two weeks.Fla. Stat. §45.031

Look up your case and sale date

  • Your case: search by your name or case number on the Duval Clerk’s CORE portal. The sale date is set in the final judgment.
  • The auction calendar: scheduled sales are listed on duval.realforeclose.com.
  • The Clerk’s overview: the Duval Clerk’s foreclosure page explains the sale process and has the owner surplus claim form.

Jacksonville help most homeowners never hear about

  • City Foreclosure Intervention Program: the City of Jacksonville offers up to $12,000 as a deferred-payment loan to owner-occupants with a documented hardship and household income under 80% of area median. Homes in Baldwin and the beaches (Jacksonville Beach, Neptune Beach, Atlantic Beach) aren’t eligible. Apply through Jacksonville Area Legal Aid: (904) 356-8371 ext. 401 or jaxlegalaid.org. It’s first-come, first-served, so check whether funds are available now.
  • Free counseling and legal help: Jacksonville Area Legal Aid (126 W. Adams St., 904-356-8371) is also a HUD-approved housing counseling agency, and the Duval Clerk refers homeowners who can’t afford a lawyer there. HUD’s counselor line is 800-569-4287.
  • Your servicer: ask in writing for a reinstatement quote, a repayment plan, forbearance or a loan modification. A complete application submitted early in the case gives you the most protection.

Where selling fits

Because Florida’s process is slower, Jacksonville homeowners often have time to sell before the clerk’s sale — and a sale that closes first pays off the loan and ends the case, with any remaining equity going to you rather than to auction bidders. If you owe more than the house is worth, a short sale may be possible with the lender’s approval. We buy Jacksonville houses as-is, with no repairs, fees or commissions, and when the timeline allows we can close in as little as two weeks through a Florida title company. If keeping the house looks realistic — through the city program, a modification or a repayment plan — we’ll tell you that too.

Questions homeowners ask

How long does foreclosure take in Jacksonville?
Longer than in most states, because Florida forecloses through the courts. Servicers generally can’t start until you’re more than 120 days behind, then the lawsuit has to move through service, a 20-day response period and a final judgment before a sale is scheduled 20 to 35 days later. A contested case can take much longer; an ignored one can move quickly.
How do Duval County foreclosure auctions work?
They’re held online at duval.realforeclose.com, Monday through Friday except legal holidays, with the first sale starting at 11:00 a.m. Bidders put down a 5% deposit and the winner pays the balance the same day. The sale date comes from the final judgment.
Can I still sell my Jacksonville house during a foreclosure lawsuit?
Yes. You own the house until the sale, so you can sell it while the case is pending. A sale that closes before the clerk’s auction pays off the loan from the proceeds and the case is dismissed. The earlier you start, the easier it is to get a title search and payoff done in time.
What happens to the extra money if my house sells for more than I owe?
Any surplus after the judgment is paid sits with the Clerk. As the owner of record, you can claim it yourself with the Duval Clerk’s surplus claim form; you don’t need a lawyer or a recovery company. File before the Clerk reports it as unclaimed, which happens one year after the sale (Florida Statutes §45.032).
Is there any local help to catch up on my mortgage?
Possibly. The City of Jacksonville’s Foreclosure Intervention Program offers income-qualified owner-occupants up to $12,000 as a deferred-payment loan, applied for through Jacksonville Area Legal Aid. Funding is first-come, first-served, so call early: (904) 356-8371 ext. 401.
