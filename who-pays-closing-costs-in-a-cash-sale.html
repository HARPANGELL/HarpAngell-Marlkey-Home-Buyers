Who Pays Closing Costs in a Cash Sale?

“We pay typical closing costs” is on almost every cash buyer's website, including ours. Here's exactly what that covers in a Georgia closing — and what it never can.

Published October 26, 2026 · MarKey Home Buyers

Short answer: in a direct cash sale, the buyer typically covers the usual closing costs, and that's what MarKey does. But “closing costs” is a narrower category than many sellers think, so it's worth knowing what's actually on the list, who the law says pays, and which items come out of your proceeds no matter who's buying.

What closing costs exist in a Georgia cash sale

Georgia is an attorney-closing state: the State Bar's position is that a Georgia attorney must be involved in preparing and executing the deed, so even a cash closing with no lender runs through a closing attorney. That means a cash closing still has an attorney's settlement fee, a title search and examination, usually an owner's title insurance policy, recording fees, and Georgia's transfer tax: $1.00 for the first $1,000 of the sale price plus 10 cents for each additional $100 — about $1 per $1,000, so roughly $250 on a $250,000 sale.

What a cash sale doesn't have is everything lender-related, which is much of what makes closings expensive: no loan origination fee, no points, no appraisal fee, no lender's title policy. That's a big part of why cash closings tend to be cheaper and faster for everyone at the table.

Who pays by law, and who pays by custom

Under Georgia law, the seller is liable for the transfer tax, but the parties can agree in the contract that the buyer pays it — and in practice nearly every line item (attorney fee, title work, transfer tax) is negotiable, with the contract controlling. In a traditional financed sale, the buyer usually pays the loan-related costs and the parties split or negotiate the rest, with property taxes prorated between buyer and seller as of the closing date.

In a direct cash sale the custom is simpler: the buyer covers the typical closing costs as part of the offer. When MarKey makes you an offer, there's no commission and no fee deducted from it — what comes out of your proceeds is only your own payoffs and prorations, and we'll show you the estimated settlement statement before closing so you can see your actual net.

What “we pay closing costs” can never cover

No buyer can pay off your obligations for you — those come out of your sale proceeds with any buyer, cash or financed. That includes your remaining mortgage balance, any tax liens or other liens on the property, your share of this year's property taxes prorated up to closing day, past-due HOA dues, and any water or utility balances that attach to the property. These aren't closing costs; they're your debts being settled from your money. A trustworthy buyer will walk you through an estimated settlement statement before closing — and you should ask for one.

One thing to watch for

Some operators advertise “no closing costs” and then deduct a vague “service fee” or “transaction fee” from the price at closing. Whoever you sell to, compare the offer price to the net to seller line on the settlement statement. If those numbers differ by more than your own payoffs and prorations, ask why before you sign. Every closing is different — contract terms control who pays what, and figures above are illustrations, not quotes. This is general information, not legal, tax, or financial advice. For questions about a specific closing, talk to a Georgia real estate attorney.
