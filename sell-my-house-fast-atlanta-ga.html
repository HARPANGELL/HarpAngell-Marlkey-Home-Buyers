Foreclosure in Fulton and DeKalb

Most Georgia home loans are foreclosed under a power of sale, without a court case. The sale is held the same way as sheriff's sales: on the first Tuesday of the month at the county courthouse, meaning the front steps of the Fulton County Courthouse at 136 Pryor Street, or the DeKalb County Courthouse in Decatur, unless a superior court order designates another spot. The house can only be sold at the place named in the ad, in person, not online.

Your lender must send a notice at least 30 days before the sale, by registered or certified mail or statutory overnight delivery, naming who has full authority to modify your loan. Under federal rules, foreclosure generally can't start until you are more than 120 days behind.

A sale that closes before the first Tuesday pays off the loan, so the foreclosure sale doesn't happen, as long as the price covers your payoff and closing is finished in time. Call your servicer, and talk to a free HUD-approved housing counselor about reinstatement, a repayment plan or a loan modification. See foreclosure in Georgia and behind on mortgage payments in Georgia.

Back property taxes

Property taxes are collected by the Fulton County Tax Commissioner or the DeKalb County Tax Commissioner, depending on your parcel. In DeKalb, an account unpaid after December 31 is subject to a tax lien, called a FiFa, which the Tax Commissioner calls the first step toward a tax sale. Penalties and interest keep adding up.

Georgia tax sales are sheriff's sales held on the first Tuesday at the courthouse; DeKalb holds them monthly on the courthouse steps, generally April through December. After a tax sale, you can redeem within 12 months by paying the sale price, later taxes and a 20% premium for the first year.

We buy houses with back taxes. The closing attorney pays the Tax Commissioner from the sale proceeds and the lien is released. See selling a Georgia house with tax liens.

Code violations in the City of Atlanta

Atlanta code complaints run through ATL311 (3-1-1 or 404-546-0311). The Department of City Planning enforces building and zoning codes, issues stop-work orders for unpermitted work, and writes citations when violations aren't corrected.

For unfit or unsafe houses, Georgia law lets the local government file an in rem complaint against the property. A court hearing follows 15 to 45 days after filing, and the court can order the house repaired or demolished. If the government does the work, the cost becomes a lien that ranks ahead of every lien except taxes and is collected like property taxes.

We buy houses with open violations and condemned or unsafe-structure orders. The violations come with the house, and recorded code liens are settled at closing. See selling a Georgia house with code violations.

Probate and inherited houses

When the owner has died, the estate usually goes through the probate court of the county where they lived: the Fulton County Probate Court at 136 Pryor Street SW, or the DeKalb County Probate Court in Decatur. The court probates the will and appoints the executor or administrator who can sign. Read how probate works in Georgia.

We buy inherited houses full of belongings. Talk to a Georgia probate or real estate attorney about who can sign. See selling an inherited house in Georgia and selling a house in probate.

Title problems

Georgia deeds and liens are filed with each county's Clerk of Superior Court. Common Atlanta problems: the house is still in a deceased parent's name, an old loan or judgment was never released, or an heir can't be found. We review title before we make an offer and work through what we find with the closing attorney, and we'll tell you up front if something will take longer.
