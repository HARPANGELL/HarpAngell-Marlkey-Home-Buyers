The house is still in Grandma's name. One of your brothers lives in Texas and won't return calls, and nobody's sure how many cousins have a share. Or the closing attorney found a mortgage from years ago that was paid off but never released. However it showed up, a title problem can freeze a sale cold, and it's easy to feel like the house is locked up for good. Often it isn't, and you don't have to untangle it yourself.

Why a title problem stops a normal sale

Before any Georgia closing, a title examiner searches the county deed and lien records. A title insurance company then decides whether it will insure the new owner. Anything in the record that leaves ownership in doubt, or leaves a debt unpaid, is a "cloud" on the title. Until it's cleared, the title company won't issue a clean policy, and without title insurance, almost no buyer's lender will fund the loan. That's why so many retail sales fall apart late: the buyer is ready, but the title isn't.

Common Georgia title problems

  • The owner died and the house is still in their name. No one has legal authority to sign a deed until probate, a no-administration order, or another court step gives it to someone.
  • Heirs property with many heirs. When a house passes down without a will over a generation or two, ownership can be split among many relatives, each with a fractional share.
  • Missing or uncooperative heirs. One heir who can't be found, or won't sign, can stop a sale of the whole house.
  • Old unreleased mortgages. A security deed that was paid off but never canceled of record.
  • Judgment liens and IRS liens. Court judgments against an owner, or a federal tax lien, attached to the property.
  • Deed errors. A wrong legal description, misspelled names, a missing signature or witness, or a gap in the chain of title.
  • Boundary and survey issues. Encroachments, unclear property lines, or a neighbor's fence on your lot.
  • Back taxes or a past tax sale. A recorded fi. fa. or an old tax deed. See selling a Georgia house with tax liens.

Georgia tools that can clear title

Georgia law has several tools for these problems. You don't need to learn how they work. Here's what they're called, so the words aren't a mystery when you hear them:

  • Probate and administration in the county probate court, which appoints someone with authority to sell. See how probate works in Georgia.
  • Petition for no administration necessary, when there's no will and all the heirs agree on how to divide the estate.
  • Year's support, which lets a surviving spouse or minor children ask the probate court to set property aside for them, filed within 24 months of the death.
  • Quiet title, a superior court action that settles competing or doubtful claims to the property.
  • Georgia's Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act, which adds protections when a co-owner asks a court to divide or sell heirs property: an appraisal, a chance for the other family owners to buy out that share, and rules favoring an open-market sale.
  • Releases, corrective deeds and IRS discharges, which clear old loans, fix deed errors, or release one property from a federal tax lien.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell a house with a clouded title in Georgia?
Often, yes, but usually not to a buyer who needs a mortgage until the cloud is cleared. A cash buyer that works with title attorneys can buy the house and handle the curative work. Send us the address and we can usually tell you within 24 hours how it looks.
The house is still in my late parent's name. Can I sell it?
Yes, once someone has legal authority to sign. In Georgia that usually comes through the county probate court, a petition for no administration necessary when all heirs agree, or year's support for a surviving spouse or minor children. A Georgia probate attorney can tell you which fits.
What if one heir won't agree to sell?
If the heirs own the house together, everyone usually has to sign to sell the whole property. A co-owner can ask a court for a partition, and Georgia's Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act gives family co-owners extra protections, including a chance to buy out that share.
How long does it take to clear a title problem in Georgia?
It depends on the problem. A missing release from a paid-off lender may take weeks; a quiet title action or an heirship case can take months. After the title search, we can tell you what's involved.
Do you buy houses with judgment or IRS liens?
Yes. Judgment liens are usually paid from the sale proceeds at closing, and an IRS lien can be paid or, in some cases, released from the property through an IRS certificate of discharge. The title company handles the payoffs.
Can every title problem be cleared?
No, and anyone who says otherwise isn't being straight with you. Some issues can't be insured, or would cost more to fix than the house is worth. We'll tell you honestly after we review the title.
