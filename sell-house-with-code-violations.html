It usually starts with a letter or a sticker on the door. Then comes a deadline you can't meet, a court date, and fines that keep adding up while the house sits. If the city has called the house unsafe or posted it as condemned, it can feel like you're stuck with it until it's fixed.

You may not be. An open code violation doesn't by itself stop a sale. MarKey Home Buyers looks at houses with code problems across Georgia, Florida, Texas and Tennessee, and when it's a fit, we buy them as-is with the violations attached.

Code situations we evaluate

  • Notices of violation or citations from the city or county
  • Overgrown lots, junk and debris, or boarded-up vacant houses
  • Unpermitted additions, conversions or renovations
  • Code fines or code enforcement liens recorded against the property
  • Unsafe-structure, condemned or demolition notices
  • Cleanup or board-up work the city did and billed to you

How a code case can turn into debt on the house

In Atlanta and other cities and counties, code enforcement is handled locally, so the process and the fines depend on where the house sits. The pattern is usually the same, though: a complaint or inspection, a written notice with a deadline, then citations, a hearing or court if the work isn't done.

Unpaid fines can be recorded as liens on the property. In Georgia, under the state’s unfit-buildings law, if a court orders an unsafe house repaired or torn down and the owner doesn't act, the local government can do the work itself. That cost becomes a lien that ranks ahead of every other lien except taxes. The longer a case sits, the more it can cost you.

Questions sellers ask

Can I sell a house with code violations?
Yes, in most cases. Open violations generally stay with the property, which is why many retail buyers and their lenders walk away, but a cash buyer can take the house as-is. Be upfront about any notices you’ve received, and send them to us with the address so we can account for them.
Do code enforcement fines become a lien on my house?
They can. Depending on the city or county, unpaid fines, or the cost of cleanup, repair or demolition work the local government did, can be recorded as a lien on the property. Recorded liens are typically paid from the sale proceeds at closing, and we can tell you what we see on the records.
What happens if the city condemns my house?
A condemned or unsafe-structure notice usually means no one can live in the house until it’s repaired, and in some cases a court can order repair or demolition. Don’t ignore the deadlines, and talk to the code office or an attorney about them. If selling makes sense, we can look at the house as-is and tell you what an offer would look like.
Will code violations show up on a title search?
Recorded fines and liens usually show up on a title search. Open violations that haven’t become liens may not, but the city still has them on file and they stay with the property. Tell any buyer what you know; with us, just send the notices and we’ll factor them in.
Can I sell a house with unpermitted work?
Yes. Unpermitted additions, garage conversions and renovations can scare off retail buyers and lenders, but we can look at the house as it is. Tell us what was done and when, and we’ll factor it into our offer.
