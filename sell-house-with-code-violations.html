The notices keep coming and the fines keep growing

It might be a roof, overgrown yard, a failed inspection on a rental, unpermitted work from years ago, or a fire-damaged house the city now calls unsafe. Each notice comes with a deadline, and missing it can mean daily fines, a lien recorded against the house, or even a demolition order. Fixing it all can cost more than you have. Selling the house as-is is often the cleanest way out.

How code enforcement escalates

StageWhat it usually means
Complaint and inspectionA neighbor or inspector reports an issue and the city inspects.
Notice of violationYou get a written list of problems and a deadline to fix them.
Hearing and finesMissed deadlines can lead to a hearing, fines, or daily penalties.
LienUnpaid fines or city repair costs can be recorded as a lien on the house.
Unsafe structure or demolition orderFor serious hazards, the city can order the building secured, vacated, or torn down.

Your options, in one line each

  • Make the repairs: get permits, fix the items, and pass reinspection.
  • Ask the city for more time: sometimes granted; get it in writing.
  • Sell to a buyer who takes the violations: the repairs become the buyer's job.

Do you have to disclose code violations?

Yes. Known violations and open notices should be disclosed to any buyer. With us, that's not a problem: we expect them, factor them into the offer, and take them on after closing.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell a house with open code violations?
Yes. You need to disclose them, but a cash buyer like us will buy the house as-is and take care of the repairs after closing.
Do code enforcement liens have to be paid at closing?
Recorded liens generally have to be paid off or released for a buyer to get clear title. The title company handles that from the sale proceeds at closing.
Can code enforcement take my house?
In some situations, unpaid code liens or demolition costs can lead to serious action against the property. The rules vary by state and city. Your state guide explains what applies where you live.
Can you sell a condemned house?
Yes. We buy condemned and unsafe houses as-is. Send us the address and the notice.
Does the buyer take over the violations?
When you sell to us, yes. We plan the repairs into our offer, and once we close, the violations are ours to resolve with the city.
