You're grieving, and somehow you've also become the person everyone calls about the house. It's still in Mom's or Dad's name, nobody's sure how to sell it, the property tax bill just arrived, and there may still be a mortgage. Maybe your siblings are in three different states, or don't agree on what to do. It can feel like the house is locked until "probate is over." In Georgia, it usually isn't. You can often sell long before the estate closes.

Who can sell an inherited house in Georgia?

A house can only be sold by someone with legal authority to sign the deed. In Georgia, that usually comes one of these ways:

  • An executor named in a will, once the county probate court issues letters testamentary.
  • An administrator, appointed by the probate court when there's no will.
  • All of the heirs together, after the probate court grants an order that no administration is necessary, or after the house is distributed out of the estate.
  • A surviving spouse or minor children, if the probate court sets the house aside for them as year's support.

Probate runs through the probate court of the county where your loved one lived. For the basics, see how probate works in Georgia.

Selling during probate in Georgia

An executor or administrator generally has to petition the probate court for permission (called leave) to sell estate property, unless the will or the court has already given that power. The court sends notice to the heirs or beneficiaries. If no one files a written objection in time, the court can order the sale without a hearing. When heirs disagree, or live out of state

If the heirs own the house together, everyone generally has to sign to sell the whole property. One heir can ask a court to partition the property, and Georgia's Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act gives family co-owners protections in that process, including an appraisal and a chance for the others to buy out the share. Most families would rather agree than litigate. A simple cash sale with one closing date and proceeds split by the closing statement often makes agreeing easier. Out-of-state heirs don't have to travel to Georgia. They can often sign their closing documents with a mobile notary where they live. If the title is tangled across generations, see selling a Georgia house with title problems.

The bills don't wait for probate

  • Property taxes are still due to the county tax commissioner, generally by December 20. Unpaid taxes can lead to a fi. fa. and a tax sale. See selling a house with tax liens.
  • The mortgage still has to be paid. Contact the servicer early. If payments stop, foreclosure can follow; see Georgia foreclosure options.
  • Insurance, utilities and upkeep keep coming. A vacant house can also draw code complaints.

At closing, the mortgage payoff, back taxes and any liens are paid from the sale proceeds. The estate or heirs get what's left.

Frequently asked questions

Can you sell a house before probate is complete in Georgia?
Usually, yes. Once an executor or administrator is appointed, the house can be sold, generally with the probate court's permission unless the will or court already granted that power. Selling the house and closing the estate are separate events. We can usually make an offer within 24 hours.
Do all heirs have to agree to sell an inherited house in Georgia?
It depends on who holds title. If an executor or administrator is selling with court approval, heirs get notice and can object, but each one doesn't have to sign. If the heirs own the house together, everyone generally has to sign to sell the whole property.
How long does probate take in Georgia?
It varies with the estate, but the creditor period alone takes months: notice runs once a week for four weeks, and creditors have three months after the last notice. You often don't have to wait for the estate to close to sell the house.
Who pays the mortgage and property taxes on an inherited house?
They're still owed, and the estate or heirs need to keep them current to avoid late fees, a tax sale or foreclosure. When the house sells, the payoff and any back taxes are paid from the proceeds at closing.
What is year's support in Georgia?
It's a petition a surviving spouse, or someone acting for minor children, can file in probate court to have property set aside for their support. It must be filed within 24 months of the death. Ask a Georgia probate attorney whether it fits your family.
Can I sell an inherited Georgia house if I live out of state?
Yes. Out-of-state heirs can often sign closing documents with a mobile notary where they live, so nobody has to fly in. Send us the address and we'll handle the local details.
