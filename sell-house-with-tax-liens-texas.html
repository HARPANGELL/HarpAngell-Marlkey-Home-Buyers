How delinquent property taxes work in Texas

Maybe the escrow account went away when the mortgage was paid off. Maybe the bills piled up after a death in the family, or a hard year just got away from you. Now there are letters from the tax office or a collection law firm, and you're wondering if the county can really take the house. It can, eventually. But you have more time and more options than those letters make it feel like.

Texas has no state property tax. Your local appraisal district sets the value, local taxing units set the rates, and in the big metros the county tax office collects: the Harris County Tax Office, Dallas County Tax Office, Tarrant County Tax Assessor-Collector, Bexar County Tax Assessor-Collector and Travis County Tax Office. Values come from the Harris Central, Dallas Central, Tarrant, Bexar and Travis Central appraisal districts.

A tax lien attaches to your property every January 1 and outranks a homestead claim and most other liens (Tax Code §§ 32.01, 32.05). Bills go out by October 1 or soon after, and taxes are delinquent if not paid before February 1 (§§ 31.01, 31.02). Taxes are also a personal debt of whoever owned the house on January 1 (§ 32.07).

What it costs to wait

Put the first year together: by July 1, an unpaid bill has picked up a 12% penalty plus 6% interest, before any collection penalty. That's money coming straight out of your equity.

Tax suit, tax sale, redemption: the Texas version

In Texas, delinquent taxes are collected through a delinquent tax suit and a sale of the property itself. The taxing units sue, the court enters a judgment, and the property is sold at public auction on the first Tuesday of the month between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., at the courthouse or a designated area, or through an online auction that ends at 4 p.m. if the commissioners court allows it (§ 34.01). Here's how the big counties do it:

  • Harris County: the eight constables auction properties at the Bayou City Event Center, 9401 Knight Road, Houston, on the first Tuesday.
  • Dallas County: sheriff's sales are held online on the first Tuesday of every month.
  • Tarrant County: the Tax Assessor-Collector sends bidders to Constable Precinct 3 for sale dates and registration.
  • Bexar County: west side of the Bexar County Courthouse, 100 Dolorosa, first Tuesday, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m.
  • Travis County: tax-related foreclosure notices are handled by the Travis County Tax Office; check its posted sale information for dates and format.

The redemption period isn't a grace period to stay put. Texas law says the right of redemption doesn't give the former owner any right to use or live in the property during that time (§ 34.21(h)). If a sale brings more than the judgment and costs, the excess goes to the court clerk, and you have until the second anniversary of the sale to file a claim (§ 34.04). Facing a sale date now? Can you sell a Texas house with back taxes?

Yes, and the mechanics are simple. The title company searches the title, finds the tax liens, and gets the exact amount due from the tax office, including penalties, interest and any attorney's fees or court costs if a suit was filed. At closing, those amounts are paid straight from the sale price, the liens are released, and you get what's left. You don't have to come up with the tax money first.

The same closing also pays off a mortgage, a home equity loan or other liens. If you're behind on the mortgage too, see facing foreclosure in Texas. If the house came to you after a death, selling an inherited house in Texas explains the title side.

IRS liens are a separate thing

A federal tax lien comes from unpaid federal taxes, not property taxes. It attaches to everything you own, including the house, and the IRS releases it within 30 days after the debt is paid. When you sell, the title company has to deal with it separately, through a payoff or an IRS discharge of the property. If you have one, tell us up front.

Your options, in one line each

  • Pay in full: your county tax office can give you the exact amount.
  • Installment agreement: 12 to 36 months; on a homestead, the office must offer one if you haven't had one in the past 24 months (§ 33.02).
  • Four-payment plan: for homeowners 65 or older, disabled, or qualifying disabled veterans, if started before the delinquency date (§ 31.031).
  • Tax deferral: for those same homeowners, at 5% interest instead of the regular penalties and interest (§ 33.06).
  • Property tax loan: a licensed lender pays the taxes and takes over the lien, at up to 18% a year (§ 32.06). Read every term.
  • Redeem after a sale: within the deadlines above, with the premium.
  • Sell the house: the taxes come out of the sale at closing.

Call your county tax office; they'll tell you what you owe and what plans you qualify for. Not sure which fits? Send us the address and we'll walk you through it, free.

Frequently asked questions

When are property taxes delinquent in Texas?
Texas property taxes are due when you get the bill and are delinquent if not paid before February 1 of the next year. A 6% penalty and 1% interest start on February 1 and grow each month, with the penalty reaching 12% by July 1. If your taxes are already behind, we can usually tell you within 24 hours what a sale would net you after they're paid.
Can the county take my house for back taxes in Texas?
Yes, eventually. The taxing units can sue to foreclose the tax lien any time after the taxes are delinquent, and after a judgment the house can be sold at a sheriff's or constable's sale. Even a homestead isn't protected from a tax foreclosure. Calling your tax office early gives you the most options.
How long do I have to redeem my house after a tax sale in Texas?
For a residence homestead or agricultural land, two years after the buyer's deed is recorded, with a 25% premium in the first year or 50% in the second. For most other property, it's 180 days with a premium of up to 25%. Redemption doesn't give you the right to keep living in the house during that time, so talk to a Texas real estate attorney quickly.
Can I sell my house if there's a delinquent tax lawsuit?
Usually, yes, as long as the house hasn't been sold at the tax sale yet. The payoff will include the taxes, penalties, interest and the costs of the suit, and the title company pays it from the sale. Send us the address and the case details and we'll tell you how quickly we could close.
Do I get money left over if my house sells at a Texas tax sale?
Possibly. If the sale brings more than the judgment and costs, the excess goes to the court clerk, who must notify the former owner, and you have until the second anniversary of the sale to file a claim. Selling before the tax sale usually leaves you far more control over your equity.
Do you charge fees to buy a house with back taxes?
No. We charge no fees or commissions, and we pay typical closing costs. The back taxes come out of the sale price at closing through the title company. You'll see exactly how it works in our written offer.
