Foreclosure in Duval County

Florida foreclosures go through the courts. The lender files suit and records a lis pendens, and if the judge enters a final judgment, the Duval County Clerk of Courts sells the house online at duval.realforeclose.com. Sales run Monday through Friday, except legal holidays, with the first sale starting at 11:00 a.m. Under Florida law the sale is set 20 to 35 days after the judgment unless the lender agrees to a later date, so the window after judgment is short.

Before anything else, call your servicer and a free HUD-approved housing counselor; they may know options you haven't heard about. If selling is the right move, a sale that closes before the auction pays off the loan, so the clerk's sale doesn't go forward. That needs enough time to close and a price that covers the payoff, or the lender's approval of a short sale. More on foreclosure in Florida and falling behind on mortgage payments in Florida.

A house going to auction

Two kinds of auctions take Duval houses: the clerk's foreclosure sale and the tax deed sale. Either way the house goes to the highest bidder, and any surplus left after the debt is paid does not come to you automatically; under Florida law the owner has to file a claim. If you already have a date, see what to do when your Florida house is going to auction.

Back property taxes

The Duval County Tax Collector collects property taxes. Florida taxes are due November 1 and become delinquent April 1. If they stay unpaid, the Tax Collector sells a tax certificate on the house in an online LienHub sale each spring. The certificate buyer earns interest, with a 5% minimum charge on most certificates when you pay it off. Two years after April 1 of the year the certificate was issued, the holder can apply for a tax deed, and the Duval County Clerk of Court auctions the house at duval.realtaxdeed.com.

Your options: pay the taxes, redeem the certificate, or sell and have the back taxes paid from your proceeds at closing. Not sure which fits? Send us the address and we'll walk you through it, free. See selling a Florida house with back taxes.

Code violations and city liens

In Jacksonville, the Municipal Code Compliance Division (MCCD) in the City's Neighborhoods Department enforces property codes. Complaints come in through 630-CITY, an officer investigates, and unresolved cases can bring administrative or magistrate hearing fines. The City also records nuisance and demolition liens, and no one, including the owner, may enter a condemned or unsafe structure without an access pass.

Under Florida law, fines can run every day the violation continues: up to $250 a day for a first violation and $500 for a repeat, and local governments of 50,000 or more people can adopt higher limits. A recorded fine order becomes a lien. A homestead can't be foreclosed for a code lien, but the lien stays on title until it's paid. Facing a hardship? Ask MCCD about the Jacksonville Assistance and Relief Program. We buy houses with open cases and liens; see selling a Florida house with code violations.

Probate and inherited houses

Probate here runs through the Fourth Judicial Circuit, with filings at the Duval County Clerk of Courts Probate Department, 501 W. Adams Street. Before the house can be sold, the court usually has to give someone authority over it: a personal representative in a formal administration, or an order in a summary administration. A Florida probate attorney can tell you which fits. We can make an offer early and close on the court's timeline; see selling a house in probate and selling an inherited house in Florida.

Title problems

A deed still in a late parent's name, a missing heir, an old judgment or a mortgage that was never released can stall a sale. We pull title at the start so these show up early, and the title company works through what it can before closing. When something needs an attorney or a court order, we'll tell you plainly.
