My House Is Scheduled for Foreclosure Auction - What Now?

A sale date makes it real. Until that morning, the house is still yours, and you still have options. Here they are, in the order to try them.

Published August 15, 2026 · Updated October 26, 2026 · MarKey Home Buyers

A foreclosure sale date on your notice makes everything feel final. It isn’t yet. In Georgia, the sale happens on the first Tuesday of the month at the county courthouse, between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. (O.C.G.A. §9-13-161), and until it actually happens, you still own the house. Every option below gets harder with each day that passes, so start today.

First: confirm the details

  • Find the sale date and the contact. Your notice must name the person with full authority to negotiate, amend or modify your loan (O.C.G.A. §44-14-162.2). That’s who to call.
  • Find the ad. The sale is advertised once a week for four weeks in the county’s legal newspaper. Statewide, you can search ads on Georgia Public Notice.
  • Write everything down. Dates, names, what you were told. Get anything important in writing.

Option 1: Reinstate or work it out with the servicer

If you can raise the past-due amount (missed payments, late fees and allowed legal costs), many loan documents let you reinstate and cancel the sale. Ask for the exact reinstatement figure in writing. If you can’t pay it all at once, ask about a repayment plan, forbearance or a loan modification. A complete loss-mitigation application sent more than 37 days before the sale generally has to be evaluated before the sale can go ahead (12 CFR §1024.41(g)); closer than that, ask whether they’ll postpone while they review, and get any postponement in writing.

Option 2: Get free help

A HUD-approved housing counselor costs nothing and can often reach the servicer faster than you can alone: call 800-569-4287. In metro Atlanta, the Atlanta Legal Aid Society’s Home Defense Program helps homeowners at risk of losing their homes, if you qualify.

Option 3: Sell before the sale date

A sale that closes before the first Tuesday pays the loan off from the proceeds, and the equity left after liens and costs is yours. With a sale date close, a cash sale is often the only kind of sale that can fit, because there’s no loan approval or appraisal to wait on. Whether it can close in time depends on the title search and the lender’s payoff letter, so an honest buyer will tell you up front if there isn’t enough runway. Option 4: Legal routes

Filing bankruptcy triggers an automatic stay that generally halts a foreclosure sale. It’s a serious step with long-term consequences, and sometimes the right one. If the foreclosure itself has problems — missing or late notices, the wrong party foreclosing — an attorney may be able to challenge it. Both paths need a lawyer now, not the week of the sale.

What not to do

  • Don’t ignore the notice or wait for the sale to be postponed on its own.
  • Don’t pay anyone an upfront fee to “save” your house, and don’t sign over your deed to anyone before a real closing.
  • Don’t stop talking to your servicer because someone told you to.
