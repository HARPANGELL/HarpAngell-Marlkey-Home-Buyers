My House Is Scheduled for Foreclosure Auction - What Now?

Straight answers from Georgia home buyers who deal with this every week.

An auction date makes it real: first Tuesday of the month, on the courthouse steps. Take a breath — until that morning, you still own the house, and people in exactly your position exit this every month with their equity intact. But every option below gets weaker with each passing day.

Option 1: Reinstate or work out the loan

If you can raise the full past-due amount (payments, late fees, legal costs), most loan documents let you reinstate and cancel the sale. Call the lender's attorney — the number is on your notice — and ask for the exact reinstatement figure in writing. Also ask whether the lender will postpone the sale while reviewing a modification application; they sometimes will, but get any postponement in writing.

Option 2: Sell before the sale date

A cash sale that closes before auction day pays the loan off entirely and stops the foreclosure. With two-plus weeks before the sale, this is very doable — we've closed on tighter timelines. The payoff coordination with the lender's attorney is routine for us; here's exactly how it works.

Option 3: Legal routes

Filing bankruptcy triggers an automatic stay that halts the sale — a serious step with long consequences, sometimes the right one. If the foreclosure itself has defects (wrong notices, wrong party foreclosing), an attorney may challenge it. Both paths need a lawyer now, not the week of the sale.
