Is Your Texas House Going to Auction? What Happens and What You Can Do

Whether it's a first-Tuesday trustee sale or a tax sale after a tax suit, you can still act until the auction happens.

Get My Free Cash Offeror call/text (678) 926-9776

Quick answerIn Texas, mortgage trustee sales and most tax sales happen on the first Tuesday of the month between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. at the county courthouse or a designated area, and some counties hold tax sales online. A trustee sale can be posted only 21 days ahead, and after a mortgage sale there's generally no right to buy the house back; after a tax sale, a homestead can be redeemed within two years at a 25% to 50% premium. A sale that closes before the auction pays off the debt from the proceeds, so the auction doesn't happen for that house, if there's enough time to close.

Two kinds of Texas auctions

Seeing your address on a sale list, or a date circled on a certified letter, is frightening. Here's the first thing to know: until the auction actually happens, the house is still yours, and you can still sell it. The second thing to know is which kind of auction you're facing, because the rules are different.

If the first Tuesday is January 1 or July 4, both kinds of sale move to the first Wednesday (§ 51.002(a-1); § 34.01).

What happens on auction day

At a trustee sale, the trustee reads the sale out in the designated area and takes bids. In Bexar County, for example, mortgage and tax sales are both cash auctions, paid in cash or certified funds. The highest bidder gets a trustee's deed. If you're still living there, the new owner may file an eviction case to take possession.

At a tax sale, an officer auctions the property under the court's order. In Harris County, the eight constables run their auctions at the same time at the Bayou City Event Center, 9401 Knight Road; Dallas County holds its sheriff's tax sales online on the first Tuesday. The buyer gets a deed subject to your right of redemption, and Texas law says that right doesn't let you keep using or living in the house during the redemption period (§ 34.21(h)).

How much time is left

Usually less than it feels like:

  • A trustee sale can be posted as little as 21 days ahead, and it must start at the time in the notice or within three hours after it (§ 51.002(c)).
  • Federal rules protect a borrower who sends the servicer a complete loss-mitigation application more than 37 days before a sale (12 CFR 1024.41(g)). Inside that window, that protection may not apply.
  • For a tax sale, the tax office or the collection law firm on your notice can tell you exactly what's owed.

Keep talking to your servicer or the tax office, and a free HUD-approved housing counselor can help with a mortgage sale; HUD's counselor search lists agencies.

What's at stake: your equity

At an auction, the house goes to the highest bidder, and that bid doesn't have to match what the house is worth. Whatever equity you had above the debt is only returned to you if the bid is high enough to leave a surplus, and only if you claim it. After a mortgage sale, if the bid is less than you owe, the lender can sue for the difference within two years (§ 51.003). After a tax sale, redeeming a homestead costs the buyer's price plus a 25% to 50% premium. Selling before the sale is usually the way to keep the most of what's yours.

What a fast cash sale before the auction can do

If a sale closes before the auction, the title company pays off the loan or the delinquent taxes from the sale price at closing, so the auction doesn't happen for that house. You get whatever is left after the payoffs.

That depends on one condition: enough time to close. We can close in as little as 14 days, but the title company needs a payoff statement, and title has to be clear enough to insure. If your sale is days away, we'll tell you honestly whether we can close in time. Whether a lender agrees to postpone a sale while a closing is pending is the lender's decision, though a signed purchase contract gives you something concrete to show them. We can't promise a sale will be stopped; we can promise a straight answer, fast.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell my house before the foreclosure auction in Texas?
Yes. You own the house until the auction happens, so you can sell it, and the loan is paid off from the sale at closing. The question is whether there's time to close before the sale date. Send us the address and the date and we'll usually tell you within 24 hours.
What day are foreclosure auctions held in Texas?
Mortgage trustee sales are held on the first Tuesday of the month between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., at the county courthouse or an area the commissioners court designates. Tax sales follow the same first-Tuesday schedule, and some counties, such as Dallas County, hold them online. If the first Tuesday is January 1 or July 4, sales move to the first Wednesday.
Can I get my house back after a Texas tax sale?
For a residence homestead or agricultural land, you can redeem within two years after the buyer's deed is recorded by paying the buyer's costs plus a 25% premium in year one or 50% in year two. For most other property, the period is 180 days. Talk to a Texas real estate attorney quickly, because redemption doesn't let you stay in the house meanwhile.
What happens to extra money from a Texas foreclosure or tax sale?
If a trustee sale brings more than the debt and costs, the excess pays other lienholders first, and any remainder may be available to you if you claim it. After a tax sale, the excess goes to the court clerk, and you must file a claim before the second anniversary of the sale. Selling before the auction usually leaves you much more.
How fast can you close before an auction?
As soon as 14 days in many cases, depending on how quickly the lender sends a payoff and whether the title is clear. If the sale is very close, we'll tell you honestly whether we can make it. Call or text (678) 926-9776 with the date.
Will the lender postpone the sale if I'm selling the house?
Sometimes, but it's the lender's decision, not ours or yours. A signed purchase contract with a closing date gives you something concrete to send your servicer when you ask. Keep talking to them either way.
