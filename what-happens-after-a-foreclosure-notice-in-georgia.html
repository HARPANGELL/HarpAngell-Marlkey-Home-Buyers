What Happens After a Foreclosure Notice in Georgia?

A foreclosure notice is the start of a process, not the end of one. Here's what it means, how fast things move from here, and what you can still do.

Published August 15, 2026 · Updated October 26, 2026 · MarKey Home Buyers

That letter from the lender’s attorney is designed to get your attention — and it should. But a foreclosure notice in Georgia is the start of a short process, not the end of one. Understanding the timeline is the difference between choosing your outcome and having one chosen for you.

What the notice actually means

Georgia is a non-judicial foreclosure state: for most home loans, the lender doesn’t have to sue you to foreclose. It uses the power of sale in your security deed instead. Federal rules generally keep the servicer from starting until the loan is more than 120 days delinquent (12 CFR §1024.41(f)). After that, Georgia law requires the lender to:

  • send you written notice of the sale at least 30 days before the sale date, by certified or registered mail or overnight delivery (O.C.G.A. §44-14-162.2);
  • name in that notice the person with full authority to negotiate, amend or modify your loan; and
  • advertise the sale once a week for four weeks in the county’s legal newspaper (§44-14-162; §9-13-140).

The law requires the lender to tell you who can negotiate — it doesn’t require the lender to agree to a modification. But that name is the first call to make.

The timeline from here

Foreclosure sales in Georgia happen on the first Tuesday of the month, between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., at the county courthouse (O.C.G.A. §9-13-161). Once the notice arrives, you may have five weeks or less. That’s among the fastest timelines in the country, but it’s still enough time to act if you start now. After the sale there’s no general right to buy the house back.

Your realistic options right now

  • Reinstate: ask for a written figure to bring the loan current, including fees.
  • Work it out: a repayment plan, forbearance or modification. Send a complete application as early as you can; one sent more than 37 days before the sale generally has to be evaluated before the sale goes ahead (12 CFR §1024.41(g)).
  • Get free help: a HUD-approved housing counselor (800-569-4287), and in metro Atlanta the Atlanta Legal Aid Society if you qualify.
  • List the house, if it will show well and there’s time for a financed buyer to close.
  • Sell directly to a cash buyer like MarKey Home Buyers. When the timeline allows, a cash sale can close before the sale date, pay off the loan at closing and put the remaining equity in your pocket.
  • Talk to an attorney if you’re considering bankruptcy or think the notice or ads were defective.

Doing nothing is the only option with no upside: fees keep stacking, equity erodes, and the sale date arrives either way. For where sales happen in Fulton and DeKalb and how to look up your ad, see our Atlanta foreclosure guide.

This is general information, not legal advice. Foreclosure defense attorneys and HUD-approved housing counselors offer free or low-cost help — use them.
