Selling a House With Back Taxes or Tax Liens in Georgia

Unpaid Georgia property taxes can lead to a fi. fa., a courthouse tax sale and a tax deed. You can still sell, and the taxes get paid at closing.

Maybe the tax bill came in a year when money was tight, or the house is inherited and nobody realized the taxes stopped getting paid. Now there are letters from the tax commissioner, maybe a notice about a "fi. fa.," and you're wondering whether the county can take the house. In Georgia, it can eventually, but it's a long road, and you can sell before that happens.

How delinquent property taxes work in Georgia

Who collects. In Georgia, the county tax commissioner collects county and school property taxes, and in some counties city taxes too. That's the Fulton, DeKalb, Cobb or Gwinnett County Tax Commissioner in metro Atlanta, and the Chatham (Savannah), Richmond (Augusta), Muscogee (Columbus), Bibb (Macon) and Clarke (Athens) County Tax Commissioners elsewhere.

When taxes are late. Georgia property taxes are generally due December 20, unless the county sets an earlier date or bills in installments. Once the due date on your bill passes, the balance is past due and interest starts to build.

What it costs to wait. Interest is added every month a balance remains, at a rate set by state law. On most bills, state law adds a 5% penalty after 120 days, and another 5% every 120 days after that, up to 20% (homestead tax bills of $500 or less are exempt from the penalty).

The fi. fa. (tax lien). After the deadline, the tax commissioner sends a written notice. Thirty days after that notice, it can issue a tax execution, called a fi. fa. (fieri facias). The fi. fa. is recorded on the general execution docket with the clerk of superior court, becomes a public lien on the property, and can show up on credit reports. In some counties, older fi. fas. were transferred to private investors; if yours was, the payoff comes from that investor, not the county.

From fi. fa. to tax sale to tax deed

StepWhat it means in Georgia
Tax lien / fi. fa.The recorded tax execution. It lets the sheriff levy on the property.
Levy and advertisingThe property is advertised for four weeks in the county legal organ before the sale.
Tax saleA public auction at the courthouse, usually on a first Tuesday: in Atlanta on the Fulton County Courthouse steps at 136 Pryor Street SW, in Decatur at the DeKalb County Courthouse, and in front of the Gwinnett Justice & Administration Center in Lawrenceville.
Tax deedThe high bidder gets a sheriff's tax deed, which is "defeasible": it can still be undone if you redeem.
RedemptionFor 12 months after the sale, and after that until the buyer ends the right to redeem. The price is the sale amount plus taxes the buyer paid, plus a 20% premium for the first year (or part of a year) and 10% for each year after.
Barment noticeAfter 12 months, the buyer can serve notice and publish it weekly for four weeks to cut off redemption on a stated date.
Excess fundsIf the bid beats the taxes and costs, the extra is held for the owner and lienholders. Notice goes out within 30 days; unclaimed funds go to the state after five years.

How long until someone can take the house? It takes a while, but the cost climbs the entire time. The tax sale comes after the fi. fa. and the levy. Then the owner has at least 12 months to redeem, and the buyer must serve and publish a barment notice before the right ends. Every month adds interest before the sale, and every year after it adds to the redemption premium.

Can you sell a house with back taxes in Georgia? Yes

Back taxes don't stop a sale. They get paid out of it. When you sell, the title search turns up every recorded fi. fa. and unpaid tax year. The closing attorney or title company gets an exact payoff from the tax commissioner (or from the investor holding a transferred fi. fa.), pays it from your sale proceeds at closing, and the lien is marked satisfied. You don't need cash in hand to clear it first.

If the property already sold at a tax sale and the redemption right is still open, a sale may still be possible: the redemption amount is paid at closing, and the title company makes sure the tax deed is cleared. Timing matters here, so talk to a Georgia real estate attorney if the 12-month mark has passed or you've received a barment notice. If a sale date is already set, see what you can still do before a Georgia auction.

IRS liens are a separate issue

A federal tax lien from the IRS for unpaid income or payroll taxes is different from a county property-tax lien. It attaches to all of your property, not just the house, and the IRS files a public Notice of Federal Tax Lien. Selling a house with an IRS lien is often still possible: the lien can be paid from the proceeds, or the IRS can issue a discharge for that one property. A title company will handle the payoff or discharge request.

Your options

  • Pay in full: the tax, interest, penalties and fees cancel the fi. fa.
  • Partial payments: many tax commissioners accept them, but interest and penalties keep growing on the balance. Ask your county's office.
  • Redeem after a tax sale: pay the redemption amount before the right to redeem ends.
  • Sell: list, or sell for cash and let the closing pay the taxes.

Call your county tax commissioner to get the exact amount owed. If the house was inherited and the taxes slipped during probate, see selling an inherited house in Georgia. Not sure which option fits? Send us the address and we'll walk you through it, free.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell my house if I owe back property taxes in Georgia?
Yes. The title search finds the unpaid taxes and any fi. fa., and the closing attorney or title company pays them from the sale proceeds at closing. You don't need cash to clear them first. We can usually make an offer within 24 hours.
How long do you have to redeem a house after a tax sale in Georgia?
At least 12 months from the sale, and after that until the tax-sale buyer serves and publishes a notice ending the right to redeem. The redemption price includes a 20% premium for the first year or part of a year and 10% for each year after.
When are Georgia property taxes considered delinquent?
Taxes are generally due December 20 unless your county sets an earlier date or installments. Interest builds once the due date passes, and on most bills a 5% penalty is added after 120 days and every 120 days after that, up to 20%.
What is a fi. fa. in Georgia?
A fi. fa. (fieri facias) is a tax execution: the recorded lien a tax commissioner issues for unpaid taxes, at least 30 days after notifying you. It's filed with the clerk of superior court and is the step that allows a levy and tax sale.
What happens to extra money from a Georgia tax sale?
If the property sells for more than the taxes and costs, the excess is held for the owner and lienholders, and notice goes out within 30 days. Gwinnett notes you don't need to pay a third party to claim it. Unclaimed funds go to the state after five years.
Is an IRS lien the same as a property tax lien?
No. A county property-tax lien is tied to the house. An IRS lien covers all your property. Both can usually be handled at closing: the property-tax lien is paid off, and the IRS lien is paid or discharged from that property.
