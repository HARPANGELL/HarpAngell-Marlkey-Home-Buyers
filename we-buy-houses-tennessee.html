Tennessee at a glance

If you're dealing with a deadline, these are the Tennessee rules that set the clock:

TopicHow it works in Tennessee
Foreclosure typeMostly non-judicial trustee sales under a deed of trust, with no court case
Foreclosure noticeAt least two county newspaper ads, the first at least 20 days before the sale, plus 20 continuous days posted online (Tenn. Code § 35-5-101)
Sale day and placeNo single statewide day. Monday through Saturday, between 9 a.m. and 7 p.m., at the place or online platform named in the notice (§§ 35-5-104, 35-5-109)
After a foreclosure saleTwo-year redemption right unless the deed of trust waives it, and most do (§ 66-8-101)
Property taxesCollected by the county trustee; due the first Monday in October, delinquent March 1, then 1.5% interest a month (§§ 67-1-701, 67-5-2010)
Tax salesA delinquent tax lawsuit, usually in chancery court, then a court-ordered sale by the clerk, live or online; no tax certificates (§ 67-5-2501)
After a tax saleUp to one year to redeem from the order confirming the sale, shorter for long delinquencies or vacant houses, plus 12% a year interest (§ 67-5-2701)
Typical timelineForeclosure: earliest sale a little over four months after the first missed payment. Taxes: the lawsuit usually comes about a year after delinquency

This is general information, not legal advice. If you're facing a sale or a lawsuit, talk to a Tennessee real estate attorney, and on a mortgage problem, call your servicer and a free HUD-approved housing counselor too.

Why Tennessee sellers choose a cash sale

A cash sale isn't right for everyone. If your house is in good shape and you have months to wait, listing with an agent may bring a higher price. A cash sale tends to make sense when:

  • Time is short. A Tennessee trustee sale can be advertised just 20 days out, and a cash closing doesn't wait on a buyer's mortgage approval.
  • The house needs work. Roof, foundation, fire or water damage, or years of deferred repairs.
  • There's a problem attached. Back taxes, code liens, title issues or a probate estate that would scare off a regular buyer.
  • You want certainty. No showings, no inspection haggling, no deal falling through at the last minute because a buyer's loan didn't close.

Frequently asked questions

Do you buy houses anywhere in Tennessee?
We focus on the Nashville, Memphis, Knoxville and Chattanooga areas and the towns around them, and we look at houses elsewhere in Tennessee case by case. Send us the address and we'll tell you honestly whether we can make an offer.
How fast can you close on a house in Tennessee?
We can close in as little as 14 days, depending on the title, or on a later date that works for you. If there's a foreclosure or tax sale date, tell us up front so we can tell you whether we can close in time.
Do you charge fees or commissions?
No. We charge no fees or commissions, and we pay typical closing costs. The price in our written offer is what we pay for the house, less any mortgage, taxes or liens that get paid off at closing.
Do I have to make repairs before selling?
No. We buy houses as-is, including houses with major repairs, code violations or belongings left inside. You don't need to clean or fix anything.
Can you buy my house if it's facing foreclosure in Tennessee?
Often, yes. If the sale closes before the trustee sale, the title company pays off the loan from the proceeds, so the foreclosure sale doesn't happen. It depends on having enough time to close, so send us the sale date and we'll usually answer within 24 hours.
