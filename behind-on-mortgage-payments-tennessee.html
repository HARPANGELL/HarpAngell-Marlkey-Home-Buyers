Behind on Mortgage Payments in Tennessee? What Happens Next

What to expect month by month, how Tennessee's short foreclosure notice changes the math, and when selling makes more sense than catching up.

Quick answerIf you're behind on your mortgage in Tennessee, federal rules generally keep your servicer from starting foreclosure until you're more than 120 days late. Once it starts, a Tennessee trustee sale can be advertised with the first newspaper notice just 20 days before the auction (Tenn. Code § 35-5-101). That makes the first few months the time to call your servicer, talk to a HUD-approved housing counselor, and decide whether keeping or selling the house makes more sense.

What happens when you fall behind

Maybe it started with a cut in hours, a medical bill or a divorce. One missed payment became two, and now you're scared to open the mail. You're not the first person in that spot, and right now you have more options than you will later.

Here's roughly how it unfolds. Exact fees and deadlines come from your note and deed of trust, so check those papers.

How far behindWhat usually happens
First monthA late fee is charged once the grace period in your note runs out. Your servicer must try to reach you by phone by day 36 of the delinquency (12 CFR 1024.39(a)).
30 to 60 daysBy day 45 the servicer must send written information about loss mitigation options (12 CFR 1024.39(b)). Late payments can show up on your credit report, and fees keep adding up.
60 to 90 daysMost deeds of trust require a breach (default) letter before the lender can demand the whole balance. It sets a deadline to catch up.
90 to 120 daysThe loan may be referred to a foreclosure attorney or trustee. A complete loss mitigation application still has to be reviewed under federal rules.
More than 120 daysThe first foreclosure step is allowed (12 CFR 1024.41(f)). In Tennessee, that's the trustee's notice of sale.

What's different about Tennessee

The 120-day federal rule is the same everywhere. What happens after it is where Tennessee gets tough:

  • No court case. Most Tennessee foreclosures are trustee sales under the deed of trust, so there's no judge or hearing that slows things down.
  • Short notice. The sale must be advertised at least twice in a county newspaper, with the first ad at least 20 days before the sale, posted online for at least 20 continuous days, and mailed to you by registered or certified mail by the first ad date (§ 35-5-101).
  • No general right to reinstate in state law. Outside certain high-cost loans, your right to catch up and stop the process comes from your deed of trust, not a Tennessee statute.
  • Little chance to get it back after. Tennessee's two-year redemption right is waived in most deeds of trust (§ 66-8-101), and if the sale price doesn't cover the debt, the lender can sue for the difference, generally within two years (§ 35-5-117).

The takeaway: in Tennessee, the time to act is before the notice of sale, not after. If a notice has already come, go straight to facing foreclosure in Tennessee.

When selling makes sense

Keeping the house is often the goal, and it's worth fighting for when the numbers work. Selling tends to make sense when:

  • Even a modified payment wouldn't fit your budget for long.
  • The house needs repairs you can't pay for, so a retail sale would take months.
  • You have equity, and every month adds late fees, interest and foreclosure costs that eat into it.
  • Your property taxes are behind too. In Tennessee they draw 1.5% interest a month once they're delinquent (§ 67-5-2010); see selling with back taxes in Tennessee.
  • You'd rather choose your moving date than have a trustee choose it for you.

When you sell, the title company gets a payoff statement from your servicer and pays the full balance, the past-due amount and any fees from the sale price at closing. Whatever's left after the payoff and any other liens is yours. Selling before a sale is advertised gives you the most room, and you keep living in the house until closing.

Frequently asked questions

How many mortgage payments can I miss before foreclosure in Tennessee?
Federal rules generally stop a servicer from starting foreclosure until you're more than 120 days behind, which is about four missed payments. After that, Tennessee only requires the first newspaper notice of the trustee sale 20 days before the auction, so things can move fast. Call your servicer and a HUD-approved housing counselor early.
Will my lender work with me if I'm behind?
Many servicers offer repayment plans, forbearance or loan modifications, and federal rules require them to contact you early and review a complete application. Approval isn't automatic, so apply as early as you can and keep copies of everything.
Can I sell my house in Tennessee if I'm behind on payments?
Yes. The title company pays off the mortgage, including the past-due amount and fees, from the sale price at closing. You keep any money left after the payoff and other liens. We can usually make a cash offer within 24 hours.
Can I catch up after a Tennessee foreclosure notice?
Often, yes, but in Tennessee that right usually comes from your deed of trust rather than state law, so check your loan papers and ask your servicer for a reinstatement figure in writing. Talk to a Tennessee real estate attorney if the sale date is close.
Do you charge anything to make an offer?
