Where things happen in Clayton County

If your sale is tied to a foreclosure notice, an estate, back taxes or a code letter, these are the offices involved. Jonesboro is the county seat.

  • Foreclosure sales: On the first Tuesday of the month, between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., at the county courthouse in Jonesboro. The sale ad in the Clayton News names the exact spot.
  • Where sales are advertised: The Clayton News. Georgia requires the ad to run once a week for four weeks before the sale, and you must be mailed notice at least 30 days ahead.
  • Probate: Clayton County Probate Court, 121 South McDonough Street, Annex Building 3, Jonesboro (court website).
  • Property taxes: Clayton County Tax Commissioner (county tax commissioner page).
  • Code enforcement: Clayton County’s Code Enforcement Division takes complaints at 678-610-4755 or through the Click Clayton app. Cities run their own.
  • Free legal help: Atlanta Legal Aid Society’s Clayton office, 404-669-0233.

Statewide, you can also search foreclosure ads on Georgia Public Notice. A free HUD-approved housing counselor is at 800-569-4287.

Questions sellers ask

Where are foreclosure sales held in Clayton County?
On the first Tuesday of the month, between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., at the county courthouse in Jonesboro. The sale ad in the Clayton News names the exact spot. If you have a sale date, send it to us with your address and we’ll tell you honestly whether there’s time for a sale to close first.
Where is the Clayton County probate court?
Clayton County Probate Court, 121 South McDonough Street, Annex Building 3, Jonesboro. If you’re handling an estate, the house can usually be sold once the personal representative has authority.
Where are Clayton County foreclosure notices published?
In The Clayton News, the county’s legal organ, once a week for the four weeks before the sale. You can also search statewide on Georgia Public Notice. Your lender must also mail you notice at least 30 days before the sale.
Who handles property taxes in Clayton County?
Clayton County Tax Commissioner (county tax commissioner page). If back taxes are part of why you’re selling, they’re paid off from the sale proceeds at closing, like any other lien.
