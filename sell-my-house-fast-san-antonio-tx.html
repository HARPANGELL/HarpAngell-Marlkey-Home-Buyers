Foreclosure and first-Tuesday trustee sales

Most Texas foreclosures are non-judicial: no lawsuit, and a fast clock. On a homestead, the servicer must give you at least 20 days to cure the default, then post, file, and mail a Notice of Sale at least 21 days before the sale. Trustee sales happen on the first Tuesday of the month, between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., in the area the county's commissioners court designates.

In Bexar County, the County Clerk lists the sale location as the west side of the Bexar County Courthouse, between the courthouse and the Paul Elizondo Tower, unless the Commissioners Court designates another spot. Your Notice of Sale gives the earliest start time. At a trustee sale, the equity you could have sold for goes to the highest bidder that day.

A sale to us that closes before the trustee sale pays off the loan, so the foreclosure sale doesn't happen, if the payoff, title, and timing work. Not at the sale stage yet? Back property taxes and the Bexar County tax sale

The Bexar County Tax Assessor-Collector's office collects property taxes for 66 taxing jurisdictions, and the Bexar Appraisal District sets values. Taxes are due January 31; after that, penalty and interest grow every month. The Tax Office's schedule:

If you pay inPenaltyInterestTotal added
February6%1%7%
March7%2%9%
April8%3%11%
May9%4%13%
June10%5%15%
July12%6%18%, plus a 15% or 20% collection fee from July 1

If nothing is paid, a taxing unit can sue to foreclose its tax lien and sell the house at a tax sale. Bexar County tax sales are held on the first Tuesday of the month, between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., on the west side of the Bexar County Courthouse at 100 Dolorosa, and the county posts the properties on its online foreclosure map ahead of each sale. A homestead owner generally gets two years to redeem afterward (at a 25% premium in year one, 50% in year two); most other property gets 180 days.

Options: a Tax Office payment plan, a deferral if you're 65+ or disabled and it's your homestead, or selling, with the back taxes paid from your proceeds at closing. More in selling a Texas house with tax liens and what to do when your Texas house is going to auction.

Code violations

In San Antonio, Code Enforcement is part of the city's Development Services Department, and complaints come in through 311 or 210-207-6000. A code officer inspects and posts a notice with a reinspection date. If the violation isn't corrected within ten days, a citation follows: an administrative citation, a Building Standards Board hearing notice, or a municipal court summons. The City can also mow, clear, secure, or demolish at the owner's expense; that bill accrues 10% interest a year, and if it isn't paid within 30 days, a code lien goes on the property automatically.

We buy houses with open code cases and code liens attached; the title company pays or settles the liens at closing. See selling a Texas house with code violations.

Probate and inherited houses

When the owner has died, someone usually needs legal authority to sign before the house can be sold, often through probate court. Bexar County's three probate courts sit at 100 Dolorosa downtown and handle wills and heirship cases. We work with the executor or administrator and close on the court's timeline. Talk to a Texas probate attorney, and see selling a house in probate and selling an inherited house in Texas.

Frequently asked questions

How fast can I sell my house in San Antonio?
We usually make a cash offer within 24 hours of seeing the house, and we can close in as little as 14 days once the title is clear. If probate, a lien payoff or other title work takes longer, we close when it's ready, or on a later date you choose. Send us the address and we'll give you a realistic timeline.
Do cash home buyers in San Antonio charge fees?
We don't. MarKey charges no fees or commissions, and we pay typical closing costs. Whoever you talk to, ask for every cost in writing before you sign.
Can I sell my San Antonio house with a code lien?
Yes. We buy houses with open code cases and City code liens, as-is. Recorded liens are paid or settled through the title company at closing, so you don't have to pay them or make repairs first. Tell us what the notice says and we can usually tell you within 24 hours what we can do.
My house is on the Bexar County tax sale list. Can I still sell it?
Often, yes, if the sale can close before the tax sale. The back taxes, penalties and interest are paid from the sale proceeds at closing. Call the Bexar County Tax Office to confirm your payoff, and send us the address so we can tell you how fast we can move.
Can I sell an inherited house in San Antonio before probate is finished?
Usually someone needs legal authority to sign first, often an executor or administrator appointed by one of the Bexar County probate courts. We can work with that person and plan the closing around the court's timeline. Talk to a Texas probate attorney about your family's situation, and send us the address when you're ready.
