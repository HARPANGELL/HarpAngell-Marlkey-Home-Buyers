Selling an Inherited House in Tennessee

How Tennessee probate and heirship affect the sale, what bills keep coming, what to do when heirs disagree or live far away, and how to sell the house as-is.

When a family member dies and the house gets stuck

The funeral is over, the mail keeps coming to the house, and the family can't agree on what to do. Maybe you live in another state. Maybe the house needs work nobody can pay for. Maybe nobody even knows whose name it's in now. You're grieving and dealing with paperwork at the same time, and that's hard.

Here's the good news: an inherited house in Tennessee can almost always be sold. It just has to be clear who can sign, and the title has to be insurable.

Tennessee probate basics

  • If there's a will: it typically goes to the probate court, which appoints the executor (the personal representative) to handle the estate. The will may give that person power to sell the house.
  • If there's no will: Tennessee real estate vests in the heirs at the moment of death, but it can still be used to pay estate debts if the other assets fall short (§ 31-2-103). Each heir owns a share, so each heir usually has to sign.
  • Creditors have a deadline. Claims against the estate generally must be filed within the period in the published notice to creditors, and no later than 12 months after the death (§ 30-2-307).
  • Small estates don't cover houses. Tennessee's small estate process is for personal property up to $50,000, not real estate (§ 30-4-102).
  • No state inheritance tax. Tennessee's inheritance tax doesn't apply to deaths after December 31, 2015. Ask a tax professional about federal taxes on the sale.

Where it happens: in Nashville, the Davidson County Probate Court (Seventh Circuit Court, Probate Division), 1 Public Square, Suite 302. In Memphis, the Shelby County Probate Court, 140 Adams Avenue, Room 124. In Knoxville and Chattanooga, it's the court that handles probate in Knox or Hamilton County. Talk to a Tennessee probate or real estate attorney about who can sign in your case. For the general process, see selling a house in probate.

The bills don't stop

While the family sorts things out, the house keeps costing money:

  • The mortgage. Federal law generally keeps a lender from calling the loan due just because the house passed to a relative at death (12 U.S.C. § 1701j-3(d)), but payments are still due. If they stop, the same foreclosure rules apply, and a Tennessee trustee sale can be advertised just 20 days ahead. See facing foreclosure in Tennessee.
  • Property taxes. Once delinquent on March 1, they draw 1.5% interest a month (§ 67-5-2010) and eventually lead to a tax suit. See selling with back taxes in Tennessee.
  • Insurance, utilities and upkeep. Check with the insurer about coverage while the house sits empty.
  • Code issues. Tall grass, debris or a break-in can bring a city notice, and unpaid cleanup costs can become a lien (§ 6-54-113).

When heirs disagree or live far away

Out-of-state heirs don't have to travel. Closing documents can usually be signed where each heir lives, in front of a notary, and sent back.

Heirs who disagree are common. One wants to keep the house, one wants to sell, one wants to rent it. Options include one heir buying out the others, or a partition case in court. For family-inherited property, Tennessee's heirs property act (for cases filed on or after July 1, 2022) gives co-owners a chance to buy out the others at a court-set value before any sale (§§ 29-27-301 to -313). A cash offer gives everyone a real number to talk about. If heirs are missing or the title is tangled, see selling with title problems in Tennessee.

Selling the inherited house as-is

Most inherited houses come with decades of belongings and repairs nobody got to. You don't have to clear it out or fix it up.

  • Leave what you don't want. Take the photos and keepsakes; we handle the rest.
  • Disclosure is simpler. Tennessee exempts sales by an estate's personal representative from the residential disclosure requirement, and also sales where the owner hasn't lived in the house at any time in the prior three years (§ 66-5-209).
  • Payoffs happen at closing. Any mortgage, back taxes or liens are paid from the sale, and the rest goes to the estate or the heirs.

How the money gets divided

Who receives the check depends on who sells. If the personal representative sells for the estate, the money goes into the estate, which generally pays administration expenses, taxes, funeral costs and other debts before distributing what's left to the heirs or the people named in the will (§ 31-2-103). If the heirs sell in their own names, each heir's share of the net proceeds generally follows their ownership share, after the mortgage, taxes and liens are paid at closing. A Tennessee probate attorney can confirm how it works for your family, and a written cash offer lets everyone see the real numbers before anyone signs.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell an inherited house before probate is finished in Tennessee?
Sometimes. It depends on whether there's a will, what authority the personal representative has, and what the title company will insure. A Tennessee probate or real estate attorney can tell you who can sign, and we can usually look at the title and give you an offer within 24 hours.
Is there an inheritance tax in Tennessee?
No, not for deaths after December 31, 2015, according to the Tennessee Department of Revenue. Federal estate or income taxes are a separate question for a tax professional.
What if my siblings don't want to sell the house?
Each heir usually owns a share, so the family has to agree, someone buys out the others, or a court decides in a partition case. Tennessee's heirs property act gives co-owners a chance to buy out the others at a court-set value. A cash offer can give everyone a real number to work from.
Who pays the mortgage on an inherited house in Tennessee?
The loan stays attached to the house, and payments are still due even though federal law generally stops the lender from calling the loan just because the owner died. If payments stop, the lender can foreclose. A sale pays the loan off from the proceeds at closing.
Can I sell the house with all the stuff still inside?
Yes. Take what matters to you and leave the rest. We buy houses as-is, belongings included, with no cleaning or repairs.
