Local situations we help Orlando homeowners with

Foreclosure in Orange County

Florida foreclosures go through the courts. The lender files suit and records a lis pendens, and if the judge enters a final judgment, the Orange County Clerk of Courts sells the house at an online auction, myorangeclerk.realforeclose.com. Check your final judgment and the Clerk's posted online sale calendar for the date and time of your sale. Under Florida law the sale is set 20 to 35 days after the judgment unless the lender agrees to a later date.

Before anything else, call your servicer and a free HUD-approved housing counselor; they may know options you haven't heard about. If selling is the right move, a sale that closes before the auction pays off the loan, so the clerk's sale doesn't go forward. That needs enough time to close and a price that covers the payoff, or the lender's approval of a short sale. More on foreclosure in Florida and falling behind on mortgage payments in Florida.

A house going to auction

Two kinds of auctions take Orange County houses: the clerk's foreclosure sale and the tax deed sale. Either way the house goes to the highest bidder, and any surplus left after the debt is paid does not come to you automatically; under Florida law the owner has to file a claim. If you already have a date, see what to do when your Florida house is going to auction.

Back property taxes

The Orange County Tax Collector collects property taxes. They're due November 1 and delinquent April 1, when a 3% minimum charge plus advertising is added, and delinquent parcels are advertised in a local newspaper once a week for three weeks. If the taxes still aren't paid, a tax certificate is sold at the Tax Collector's online LienHub sale, held by about June 1 each year, with interest bids starting at 18%. If the certificate isn't redeemed within two years, the holder can file a tax deed application, and the house can be auctioned online at orange.realtaxdeed.com through the Orange County Comptroller.

Your options: pay the taxes, redeem the certificate, or sell and have the back taxes paid from your proceeds at closing. Not sure which fits? Send us the address and we'll walk you through it, free. See selling a Florida house with back taxes.

Code violations and city liens

Inside Orlando city limits, the City's Code Enforcement Division sends an inspector within 2 business days of a complaint. If there's a violation, the owner gets instructions to correct it, and for overgrown yards or trash the City may hire a contractor at the owner's expense. If it isn't corrected, the City assesses daily fees, a case can go before the Code Enforcement Board, and unpaid fees become a lien. A Code Enforcement Board lien can't be paid off while the case is still open. Outside city limits, in places like Pine Hills, Orange County handles code enforcement.

Under Florida law, fines can run up to $250 a day for a first violation and $500 for a repeat, and local governments of 50,000 or more people can adopt higher limits. A homestead can't be foreclosed for a code lien, but the lien stays on title until it's paid. We buy houses with open cases and liens; see selling a Florida house with code violations.

Probate and inherited houses

Probate here runs through the Ninth Judicial Circuit, with filings at the Orange County Clerk of Courts Probate Division, 425 N. Orange Avenue. Before the house can be sold, the court usually has to give someone authority over it: a personal representative in a formal administration, or an order in a summary administration. A Florida probate attorney can tell you which fits. We can make an offer early and close on the court's timeline; see selling a house in probate and selling an inherited house in Florida.

Title problems

A deed still in a late parent's name, a missing heir, an old judgment or a mortgage that was never released can stall a sale. We pull title at the start so these show up early, and the title company works through what it can before closing. When something needs an attorney or a court order, we'll tell you plainly.
