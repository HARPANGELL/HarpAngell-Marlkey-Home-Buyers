How foreclosure works in Tennessee

If a certified letter says your house will be sold at a "substitute trustee's sale," it's normal to feel like the floor just dropped. Take a breath. Until the sale actually happens, you still own the house, and you still have choices.

Most Tennessee home loans are secured by a deed of trust with a power of sale. That lets the lender foreclose without a lawsuit, through a public auction run by a trustee (often a substitute trustee the lender appoints). Judicial foreclosure exists in Tennessee, but most foreclosures here are these non-judicial trustee sales, and there's no judge setting the schedule.

The notices and the timeline

Federal servicing rules generally bar your servicer from making the first foreclosure notice or filing until you're more than 120 days behind (12 CFR 1024.41(f)). After that, Tennessee moves quickly, because the state's notice period is short.

PostponementsAllowed up to one year from the original date; if a delay runs more than 30 days, a new notice must be mailed to you at least 10 days before the new date (§ 35-5-101(e))
After the saleA two-year right to redeem exists unless your deed of trust waives it, and most do (§ 66-8-101)

Put together, the earliest a sale can usually happen is a little over four months after the first missed payment: more than 120 days, plus at least 20 days of notice. Many take longer. But once the notice of sale arrives, you may have only about three weeks.

Where and when Tennessee trustee sales happen

Tennessee doesn't have one statewide sale day or a fixed sale spot in each county. The notice must state the time and place of the sale (§ 35-5-104), and since 2025 the "location" can be a physical place or the website of an online bidding platform (§ 35-5-101(g)). Whether your house is in Nashville (Davidson County), Memphis (Shelby County), Knoxville (Knox County) or Chattanooga (Hamilton County), the notice is what tells you where to look:

  • The newspaper notice in your county names the date, time and place, or the bidding website.
  • The online posting must be on a posting company's website that the notice identifies (§ 35-5-104(a)(7)). Those companies have to register their web address with the Tennessee Secretary of State (§ 35-5-101(f)).
  • Postponements are generally announced through that internet posting, unless the delay is under five days, so check it again before the date.

Your options before the sale

  • Reinstate: pay what's past due plus fees. Tennessee law doesn't give a general right to reinstate except for certain high-cost loans, but many deeds of trust do.
  • Repayment plan or forbearance: catch up over time, or pause payments through a hardship.
  • Loan modification: change the terms. A complete application received more than 37 days before the sale generally must be decided before the sale goes forward (12 CFR 1024.41(g)).
  • Refinance: possible if your credit and equity still allow it.
  • Sell: list with an agent if there's time, or sell for cash if there isn't.
  • Short sale or deed in lieu: if you owe more than the house is worth, with the lender's approval.
  • Bankruptcy: filing triggers an automatic stay that pauses the sale. Talk to a Tennessee bankruptcy attorney first.

Selling before the trustee sale

You can sell right up until the auction, because the house is still yours. When you sell, the title company orders a payoff statement from your servicer and pays the loan, the past-due amount and the fees from the sale price at closing. A sale that closes before the auction pays off the loan, so the foreclosure sale doesn't happen. Behind on property taxes too? Those are paid the same way; see selling a Tennessee house with back taxes.

Timing is the condition that matters. We can close in as little as 14 days, but payoff letters and title work take time, so the more days you have before the sale, the more room there is. Tell us the date up front and we'll be honest about whether a closing can happen first. Whether a servicer agrees to postpone a sale while a closing is pending is the servicer's decision.

What's at stake if the auction happens

At a trustee sale the house goes to the highest bidder, and the lender can usually bid up to what it's owed. Any equity above the debt comes back to you only if a bid is high enough to leave a surplus. If it is, the surplus is yours after the sale costs and other liens are paid, but Tennessee's trustee-sale statutes don't lay out a claims process, so you'd have to ask the trustee for it, ideally with a Tennessee real estate attorney.

If the house sells for less than you owe, the lender can sue for the difference (a deficiency), generally within two years of the sale. Tennessee presumes the sale price was fair market value unless you prove it was materially lower (§ 35-5-117). If your date is close, read what to do when a Tennessee house is going to auction.

Frequently asked questions

How long does foreclosure take in Tennessee?
Federal rules generally keep the first foreclosure step from happening until you're more than 120 days behind. After that, a Tennessee trustee sale can be advertised with the first newspaper notice just 20 days before the sale, plus a 20-day online posting. So the earliest sale is usually a little over four months after the first missed payment, and once the notice comes you may have about three weeks. If you have a date, we can usually tell you within 24 hours whether a cash sale can close first.
Can I sell my house if a trustee sale is scheduled in Tennessee?
Yes. You own the house until the sale happens, so you can sell it, and the title company pays off the loan from the proceeds at closing. Whether there's enough time depends on the sale date and the title. Send us the address and the date and we'll give you an honest answer.
Where are foreclosure auctions held in Tennessee?
There's no single statewide location or day. The notice of sale must name the time and place, which can be a physical location or an online bidding website, and sales run Monday through Saturday between 9 a.m. and 7 p.m. Check the newspaper notice and the online posting site it names, since postponements are posted online too.
Do I get money back if my house sells for more than I owe at a Tennessee foreclosure?
If the winning bid is more than the debt, sale costs and other liens, the surplus belongs to you, but you generally have to ask the trustee for it, and the lender can usually bid up to what it's owed. Selling before the sale is usually how you keep the most of your equity.
Can I get my house back after a Tennessee foreclosure sale?
Tennessee law gives a two-year right of redemption, but deeds of trust can waive it and most do. If the IRS has a lien on the house, it has its own 120-day right to redeem after the sale. Talk to a Tennessee real estate attorney about your specific deed of trust.
Does MarKey charge fees to buy a house in foreclosure?
No. We charge no fees or commissions and pay typical closing costs. The payoff to your lender comes out of the sale price at closing, and you never sign the house over before closing. We can usually make you an offer within 24 hours.
