Facing Foreclosure? Know Your Options

Selling before the sale date is one option. It can pay off the loan, protect the equity you have left, and limit the damage to your credit.

Falling behind with the bank is more common than most people realize — a job loss, medical bills, or one hard year is all it takes. In Georgia, Texas and Tennessee, most foreclosures happen out of court and can move fast once the process starts. Florida goes through the courts and usually takes longer. The worst thing you can do is wait. The good news: until the day of the sale, you still have options — and selling is one of them.

Your lender and a free HUD-approved housing counselor (800-569-4287) can also walk you through options like reinstatement, a repayment plan or a loan modification. Talk to them too; selling is one option, not the only one.

Why homeowners in pre-foreclosure sell to us

  • Keep your equity — at auction, your equity often evaporates. Selling first means the difference goes to you, not the bank.
  • Protect your credit — a sale hurts far less than a completed foreclosure, which can follow you for seven years.
  • No cost to you — no fees, no commissions, no repairs, and we handle the paperwork and payoff coordination.

How foreclosure works in your state

  • Georgia: mostly out of court. The lender must mail notice at least 30 days before the sale and advertise it once a week for four weeks. Sales are on the first Tuesday of the month at the county courthouse.
  • Texas: mostly out of court. For a home, the servicer must give at least 20 days to catch up before a sale notice, and the sale notice comes at least 21 days before the sale. Sales are on the first Tuesday of the month, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., at the county courthouse.
  • Tennessee: mostly out of court. The notice of sale runs in a local newspaper three times, the first at least 20 days before the sale.
  • Florida: through the courts. The lender files a lawsuit; after a final judgment, the clerk sells the house at a public auction, often online, 20 to 35 days later.

In every state, federal rules generally keep a servicer from starting foreclosure until the loan is more than 120 days behind. Whatever your state, send us the address and your dates, and we’ll tell you honestly how much time there is.

Questions sellers ask

How long does foreclosure take?
Federal rules generally keep a servicer from starting foreclosure until the loan is more than 120 days behind. After that it depends on the state: Georgia, Texas and Tennessee mostly foreclose out of court, so a sale can come within weeks of the first notice, while Florida goes through the courts and usually takes longer. If you already have a date, send it to us.
When are foreclosure sales held?
In Georgia and Texas, sales are held on the first Tuesday of the month at the county courthouse. In Tennessee, the date is set in a notice published in the local paper. In Florida, the court sets the sale after a final judgment and the clerk runs the auction, often online. Send us the address and the sale date, and we’ll tell you honestly whether there’s time to close.
Can I sell my house if it’s in foreclosure?
Yes. You still own the house until the sale, and a sale that closes before the auction pays off the loan, so the foreclosure sale doesn’t happen. Timing is the catch, since a closing needs a title search and a payoff from your lender, so reach out as early as you can.
What should I do first after getting a foreclosure notice?
Call your lender to ask about reinstatement, a repayment plan or a loan modification, and talk to a free HUD-approved housing counselor (HUD’s line is 800-569-4287). If selling turns out to be your best option, we can show you what a cash sale would look like, with no obligation.
What happens to my equity if my house goes to a foreclosure auction?
At a foreclosure sale, the price is set by the bidding, not by you, and fees and costs pile on along the way. If there’s money left over, claiming it can take time and legal help. Selling before the sale lets you set the terms and keep what’s left after the loan and any liens are paid, and we’ll show you those numbers.
