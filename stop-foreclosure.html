Facing Foreclosure? Your Options Before the Sale Date

Whether you just got the first letter or the sale date is already set, you still have options, and a cash sale may be one of them.

Quick answerIn most cases you can still sell your house right up until the foreclosure sale. If a sale closes before the auction, the loan is paid off from the proceeds and the foreclosure sale doesn't go forward. How much time you have depends on your state: Georgia, Texas and Tennessee mostly use faster out-of-court sales, while Florida foreclosures go through the courts.

You still have a say in how this ends

A foreclosure letter can make it feel like the bank is in control and the clock is running out. The clock is real, but until the sale actually happens, the house is still yours to sell. Many homeowners wait longer than they want to because they don't know who to call or feel embarrassed. You don't need to have it figured out. You just need to know your options while there's still time to use them.

How much time do you have?

Federal rules generally stop a mortgage servicer from starting a foreclosure until you're more than 120 days behind. After that, the pace depends on your state.

StateHow foreclosure worksWhat that means for you
GeorgiaOut of court (non-judicial)One of the fastest states. Once the sale is advertised, it can happen within weeks.
FloridaThrough the courts (judicial)Slower, but once the judge enters a final judgment, the clerk's sale follows quickly.

Your options, in one line each

  • Catch up (reinstate): pay everything past due, plus fees, before the sale.
  • Work it out with your lender: a repayment plan, forbearance, or loan modification.
  • Refinance: replace the loan, if your credit and equity allow it.
  • Sell: list with an agent if there's time, or sell for cash if there isn't.
  • Short sale or deed in lieu: if you owe more than the house is worth, with the lender's approval.
  • Bankruptcy: can pause a sale; talk to a bankruptcy attorney first.

We always recommend calling your servicer and a free HUD-approved housing counselor too. Not sure which option fits you? Send us the address and we'll walk you through it, free.

Why selling before the auction protects you

At a foreclosure sale, the house goes to the highest bidder, often for far less than it's worth. You have no say in the price, and your remaining equity is at the mercy of the auction. If anything is left over after the debt, getting those surplus funds can take months and paperwork. A completed foreclosure also stays on your credit report for years.

Selling first changes that. You choose the buyer and the date. The loan is paid in full at closing, and the difference between the price and what you owe goes to you, not to an auction bidder.

Frequently asked questions

When is it too late to stop a foreclosure?
Usually when the sale actually happens. Until then, catching up, a lender workout, or a sale that closes first may still be possible. The closer you are to the sale date, the fewer options are left, so reach out as early as you can.
Can I sell my house if it's already in foreclosure?
Yes. Being in foreclosure doesn't stop you from selling. The loan is paid off from the sale at closing, as long as the sale closes before the foreclosure auction.
How many mortgage payments can you miss before foreclosure?
Federal rules generally keep servicers from starting a foreclosure until you're more than 120 days behind. After that, the timeline depends on your state. Your state guide has the details.
How fast can you buy my house before the auction?
We can close in as little as 14 days when the title is clear, sometimes faster. Send us your address and sale date and we'll tell you honestly whether the timing works.
What happens to my equity if the house sells at auction?
Any money above the debt and costs is surplus, and you may be able to claim it, but it can take time and paperwork. Selling before the auction lets you get your equity at closing instead.
Do you charge any fees?
No. We don't charge fees or commissions, and we pay typical closing costs. The offer is free and there's no obligation.
