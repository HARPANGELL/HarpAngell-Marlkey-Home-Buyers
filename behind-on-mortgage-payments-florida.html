Behind on Mortgage Payments in Florida? Here's What Happens Next

Missing a payment doesn't mean losing the house. Knowing Florida's timeline helps you choose your move while you still have the most options.

Quick answerIf you're behind on your mortgage in Florida, federal rules generally bar your servicer from starting foreclosure until you're more than 120 days delinquent. After that, the lender must sue you in circuit court and record a lis pendens, and you have 20 days after being served to file an answer. The earlier you act, whether that's a workout with your servicer or a sale, the more of your equity and options you keep.

The Florida part: a lawsuit, not a letter

Florida is a judicial foreclosure state. Mortgages are foreclosed "in equity," which means the lender files a lawsuit in circuit court and a judge decides it (Fla. Stat. § 702.01). The lender records a lis pendens in the county records (§ 48.23), and once you're served you have 20 calendar days to file a written answer, or a default can be entered against you (Duval County Clerk).

If the court enters a final judgment, the clerk sets the sale 20 to 35 days later, held online in the big metro counties (§ 45.031). More on that on our Florida foreclosure page.

Timing your paperwork matters too. If you send your servicer a complete loss mitigation application more than 37 days before a scheduled sale, federal rules generally bar it from moving for judgment or holding the sale while it reviews you (12 CFR 1024.41). That protection depends on the application being complete, so ask the servicer to confirm in writing what it still needs.

What's at stake as the case moves: late fees, interest and the lender's attorney costs are added to what you owe, and that shrinks whatever equity you'd walk away with. If the house sells at auction for less than the judgment, the court may also enter a deficiency judgment, capped for owner-occupied homes at the difference between the judgment and fair market value (§ 702.06).

Frequently asked questions

How many mortgage payments can I miss before foreclosure in Florida?
Federal rules generally keep a servicer from making the first foreclosure filing until you're more than 120 days delinquent. In Florida that first filing is a lawsuit in circuit court, followed by 20 days to answer once you're served. If you're already past that point, we can usually tell you within 24 hours what a sale would look like.
Will one late mortgage payment hurt my credit?
Servicers typically report a payment to the credit bureaus once it's 30 days past due, and the late mark can stay on your report for seven years. A payment made after the grace period but before 30 days usually brings a late fee under your note. If you can't catch up, it's worth looking at your options early.
Can I sell my house if I'm behind on payments in Florida?
Yes. Your lender's payoff, including the missed payments and fees, is paid from the sale at closing. You can sell before a lawsuit is filed or while it's pending, as long as the sale closes before the clerk's sale. Send us the address and we'll make you an offer.
What is a lis pendens in Florida?
It's a notice recorded in the county's official records that a lawsuit affecting the property is pending, such as a foreclosure. It warns buyers and lenders, but it doesn't stop you from selling. When the loan is paid off at closing, the foreclosure case and the lis pendens can be resolved.
Should I call my lender before selling?
Yes. Call your servicer and ask what workout options it offers, and consider a free HUD-approved housing counselor. Knowing your options helps you decide. We're happy to give you a cash number to compare while you do.
