If a certified letter from a law firm just told you your house will be sold on the courthouse steps, you're probably scared and short on time. That's a normal reaction, and you are not out of options. Until the sale happens, you still own the house, and many Georgia homeowners use that window to catch up, work out a deal with the lender, or sell on their own terms.

How foreclosure works in Georgia

Georgia is a non-judicial foreclosure state. Most home loans here are secured by a security deed (also called a deed to secure debt) with a power of sale, so the lender doesn't have to sue you or go before a judge. It follows the steps in Georgia law and your loan documents, then sells the house at a public auction.

Before that can start, federal servicing rules generally stop your servicer from making the first foreclosure notice or filing until your loan is more than 120 days delinquent (12 CFR 1024.41(f)). Once that window passes, Georgia's clock is short:

  • Notice of sale. The lender must send you written notice at least 30 days before the sale, by registered or certified mail or statutory overnight delivery. It must name the person or company with full authority to negotiate, amend and modify your loan, and include a copy of the sale ad. Refusing the certified letter doesn't cancel the notice.
  • Newspaper ad. The "Notice of Sale Under Power" runs once a week for four weeks in the county's legal organ, the official newspaper for legal notices.
  • Proof of who's foreclosing. The security deed, or the assignment to the foreclosing lender, must be filed with the clerk of superior court before the sale.

Where and when Georgia foreclosure sales happen

Foreclosure sales follow the sheriff's-sale schedule: the first Tuesday of the month, between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., at the courthouse in the county where the house sits. If that Tuesday is a holiday, the sale moves to the Wednesday. These are in-person courthouse auctions, and your notice and the newspaper ad give the exact spot.

  • Atlanta (Fulton County): the front steps of the Fulton County Courthouse, 136 Pryor Street SW, downtown Atlanta.
  • DeKalb County: the DeKalb County Courthouse in Decatur; your notice and the legal ad name the exact spot.
  • Cobb County: first-Tuesday sales at the Cobb County courthouse, with ads in the Marietta Daily Journal.
  • Gwinnett County: the county courthouse in Lawrenceville (Gwinnett holds its tax sales in front of the Gwinnett Justice & Administration Center), with ads in the Gwinnett Daily Post.
  • Savannah, Augusta, Columbus, Macon and Athens: the Chatham, Richmond, Muscogee, Bibb and Clarke county courthouses, on the same first-Tuesday schedule.

Your options before the sale

  • Reinstate: pay the past-due amount, fees and costs to bring the loan current.
  • Repayment plan or forbearance: spread out or pause the arrears for a temporary hardship.
  • Loan modification: change the loan terms. A complete application received more than 37 days before the sale generally means the servicer can't hold the sale while it's reviewed.
  • Refinance: possible if your credit and equity support a new loan.
  • Sell: list with an agent if there's time, or sell for cash to close before the sale date.
  • Short sale: if you owe more than the house is worth, with the lender's approval.
  • Deed in lieu: give the house back to the lender, only if it agrees.
  • Bankruptcy: filing before the sale can halt it for now. Talk to a Georgia bankruptcy attorney first.

Selling before the sale: how a cash sale pays off the loan

Yes, you can sell during a Georgia foreclosure (more in can I sell my house during foreclosure in Georgia?). When you sell, the closing attorney or title company requests a payoff statement from your lender's foreclosure attorney. At closing, the payoff is wired from the sale proceeds and the security deed is canceled. A sale that closes before the auction pays off the loan, so the foreclosure sale doesn't happen. Anything left after the payoff, other liens and closing costs is yours. We explain the mechanics in can selling my house stop foreclosure?

Timing is the catch. A closing needs a title search, a payoff letter and a signing. We can often close in about 14 days when title is clean, but if your sale is days away, call now; see my house is scheduled for foreclosure auction: what now? If you owe more than a buyer will pay, a short sale needs the lender's approval, which takes longer. If the math or the calendar won't work, we'll tell you plainly.

What happens to your equity if the sale goes ahead

At the auction, the lender often bids on its own loan, and investors bid looking for a discount. If the winning bid is more than what's owed plus costs, the extra is called surplus. No Georgia statute spells out who gets mortgage-foreclosure surplus; it's held for whoever is legally entitled to it, which can include junior lienholders such as a second mortgage, and you. When claims compete, the money may be paid into superior court so a judge can decide who gets it. Either way, you have to claim it. Read more in what happens to your equity in a Georgia foreclosure.

After a sale, the new owner can file a dispossessory (eviction) action. If the sale didn't cover the debt, the lender can only pursue a deficiency if it reports the sale to the superior court within 30 days and a judge confirms the sale brought true market value.

Frequently asked questions

How long does foreclosure take in Georgia?
Federal rules generally keep a servicer from starting foreclosure until you're more than 120 days behind. After that, Georgia only requires written notice at least 30 days before the sale and four weekly newspaper ads, so the earliest sale is roughly five months after the first missed payment. If you have a sale date, send it to us; we can usually tell you within 24 hours what's possible.
Can I sell my house after getting a foreclosure notice in Georgia?
Yes. You own the house until the sale, and a sale that closes before the auction pays off the loan from the proceeds. If the price won't cover what you owe, the lender has to approve a short sale. Send us the address and we'll run the numbers with you.
What day are foreclosure auctions held in Georgia?
On the first Tuesday of the month, between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., at the courthouse of the county where the property is. If that Tuesday is a holiday, the sale moves to the Wednesday. Your notice letter and the legal ad list the date.
Can I get my house back after a Georgia foreclosure sale?
Plan as if you can't. The Georgia Attorney General's office notes that a valid foreclosure wipes out the borrower's right to live in the house, so treat the sale date as your real deadline. If you think the foreclosure was done wrong, or want to know whether any option remains after a sale, talk to a Georgia real estate attorney right away.
Will my lender still work with me after the notice of sale?
Often, yes. Georgia requires the notice to name someone with authority to modify your loan, and a complete loss-mitigation application sent more than 37 days before the sale generally pauses the sale during review. Call your servicer and a free HUD-approved housing counselor; we're happy to give you a cash number to compare.
What happens to my equity if the house sells at auction?
If the winning bid is more than the debt and costs, the surplus is held for whoever is legally entitled to it, which can include junior lienholders and you. You have to claim it, and sometimes a court decides. Selling before the sale lets you collect your equity at closing instead.
