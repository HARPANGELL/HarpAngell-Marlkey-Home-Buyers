Can I Sell My House During Foreclosure in Georgia?

Until the foreclosure sale happens, the house is still yours to sell. Here's how it works, what it costs, and the deadlines that decide whether it's realistic.

Published August 15, 2026 · Updated October 26, 2026 · MarKey Home Buyers

Yes. In Georgia you own your house until the foreclosure sale actually happens on the courthouse steps, so you can sell it at any point before then. If a sale closes first, the loan is paid off from the proceeds — and anything left after the payoff, other liens and closing costs goes to you. The hard part isn’t the law; it’s the clock.

What a sale during foreclosure pays off

At closing, the closing attorney gets a written payoff from your servicer and pays it from the sale price. In a foreclosure that payoff is usually bigger than your regular balance, because it typically includes:

  • the unpaid principal and interest,
  • missed payments and late fees,
  • foreclosure attorney fees and costs the loan documents allow, and
  • anything the servicer advanced, such as property taxes or insurance.

Second mortgages, tax liens and judgment liens get paid from the proceeds too. What’s left is your equity. Compare that to a foreclosure sale, where the price is set by bidding, and where any surplus has to be claimed after the fact.

The deadlines that matter

Georgia forecloses out of court and fast. The lender must send you written notice at least 30 days before the sale (O.C.G.A. §44-14-162.2) and advertise it once a week for four weeks in the county’s legal newspaper; the sale itself happens on the first Tuesday of the month between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. (§9-13-161). So once the notice arrives, you may have five weeks or less.

That’s why the type of buyer matters:

  • A traditional buyer with a mortgage often needs 30–45 days or more for the loan, appraisal and underwriting, and one hiccup can push closing past your sale date.
  • A cash buyer skips the loan and appraisal, so closing depends mainly on title work and the lender’s payoff letter. With enough runway, that can be done in about two weeks.

Either way, nobody can honestly promise a closing before a specific date until the title search and payoff are in hand.

Ask about postponing the sale

If you’re under contract to sell, ask your servicer (the person named in your notice) whether it will postpone the sale to let the closing happen. Servicers sometimes agree, especially with a signed contract and a closing date, but they don’t have to — and get any postponement in writing. Separately, a complete loss-mitigation application sent more than 37 days before the sale generally requires the servicer to evaluate it before going ahead (12 CFR §1024.41(g)).
